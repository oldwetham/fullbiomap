Mesenteric lymph nodes clean lymph, a watery fluid that bathes the body's tissues. These nodes remove debris and pathogens, agents that can cause disease, from the lymph. Mesenteric lymph nodes lie in the mesentery, a band of tissue that anchors most of the small intestine. Like other lymph nodes, each one is small and shaped like a bean.
