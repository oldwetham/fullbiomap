Bladder urothelial cells keep urine from leaking out of the urinary bladder, which they line. When the bladder is empty, these cells seem to pile up on top of each other. But as the bladder fills, the lining unfolds and stretches to hold the urine. The lining is called transitional epithelium because its surface cells change shape as the bladder fills.
