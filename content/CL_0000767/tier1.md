A basophil is a white blood cell that releases chemicals to stimulate inflammation, a basic immune response. Each basophil is packed with large granules, which are stores of chemicals inside the cell. These granules release histamine, which adds to inflammation, and heparin, which works against blood clotting. Of all white blood cells, basophils are the least common, usually under one percent.
