Cone cells detect bright light and give us color vision during the day. Cones sit near the center of the retina, the light-sensing layer at the back of the eye. There are three kinds of cones, and each kind holds its own pigment, a molecule that absorbs light. The brain compares how active the three kinds of cones are to work out color.
