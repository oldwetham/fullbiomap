Taste receptor cells sense chemicals in the food we eat. These cells sit in clusters called taste buds, inside bumps on the tongue called papillae. Food molecules dissolve in saliva and bind to sensors on microvilli, the hair-like tips of these cells. Once activated, taste receptor cells release chemical signals onto sensory nerve cells.
