# Retinal rod cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Nuclei:** 1  
  “The inner segment contains the nucleus and other common organelles of a cell” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** outer segment discs: a stack of membrane-bound discs in the rod-shaped outer segment that hold the photopigment rhodopsin  
  “The rod-shaped outer segments of the rod photoreceptor contain a stack of membrane-bound discs that contain the photosensitive pigment rhodopsin.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** stacked membranous discs: hold the photopigment and are separate from the outer cell membrane  
  “In rods the photopigment is in stacks of membranous disks separate from the outer cell membrane.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000604 retinal rod cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000604)
- **Special structure:** outer segment: the specialized region where photoreception takes place  
  “The inner segment contains the nucleus and other common organelles of a cell, whereas the outer segment is a specialized region in which photoreception takes place.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** inner segment: holds the nucleus and the other common organelles of the cell  
  “The inner segment contains the nucleus and other common organelles of a cell, whereas the outer segment is a specialized region in which photoreception takes place.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** rhodopsin: the rod photopigment, most sensitive to light at 498 nm  
  “Rhodopsin, the photopigment in rods, is most sensitive to light at a wavelength of 498 nm.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** synapse with bipolar cells in the outer synaptic layer  
  “The change in membrane potential alters the amount of neurotransmitter that the photoreceptor cells release onto bipolar cells in the outer synaptic layer.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Photoreception happens in the specialized outer segment, while the inner segment houses the nucleus and the cell's other common organelles.  
  “The inner segment contains the nucleus and other common organelles of a cell, whereas the outer segment is a specialized region in which photoreception takes place.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Rods are so sensitive to light that a single photon can lead to an action potential in the rod's corresponding retinal ganglion cell.  
  “Rods are so sensitive to light that a single photon can result in an action potential from a rod’s corresponding RGC.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Strongly light-sensitive rods lie in the outer edges of the retina and detect dim light, serving mainly peripheral and night vision.  
  “Rods are strongly photosensitive and are located in the outer edges of the retina. They detect dim light and are used primarily for peripheral and nighttime vision.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.5 Vision, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-5-vision)
- **Structure and job:** Rods do not sense the color of light, so the low-light vision they provide is essentially in grayscale.  
  “However, cones cannot react to low-intensity light, and rods do not sense the color of light. Therefore, our low-light vision is—in essence—in grayscale.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** In normal sunlight the rods' rhodopsin is constantly bleached while cones are active, but in a darkened room vision depends entirely on rods.  
  “In normal sunlight, rhodopsin will be constantly bleached while the cones are active. In a darkened room, there is not enough light to activate cone opsins, and vision is entirely dependent on rods.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Activated rhodopsin starts a cascade that closes Na+ channels in the photoreceptor membrane, so the cell hyperpolarizes instead of depolarizing like most sensory neurons.  
  “This isomerization of retinal activates the rhodopsin, starting a cascade of events that ends with the closing of Na+ channels in the membrane of the photoreceptor. Thus, unlike most other sensory neurons (which become depolarized by exposure to a stimulus) visual receptors become hyperpolarized” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.5 Vision, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-5-vision)
- **Structure and job:** Light changes a photoreceptor's membrane potential, which alters how much neurotransmitter it releases onto bipolar cells in the outer synaptic layer.  
  “The photoreceptors (rods and cones) change their membrane potential when stimulated by light energy. The change in membrane potential alters the amount of neurotransmitter that the photoreceptor cells release onto bipolar cells in the outer synaptic layer.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
