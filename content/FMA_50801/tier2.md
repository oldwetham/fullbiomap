The brain's activity is the basis of a person's conscious experiences, and a specialized brain region governs homeostasis, the body's ability to keep its internal conditions constant. The brain and the spinal cord make up the central nervous system, and the brain is the part housed inside the skull. The adult brain has four major regions: the cerebrum, the diencephalon, the brain stem and the cerebellum. The cerebrum, the largest part of the brain, is divided into two halves, the cerebral hemispheres, which are connected by the corpus callosum, a thick bundle of fibers. The cerebrum's wrinkled outer layer, the cerebral cortex, is a thin sheet of gray matter, tissue containing the cell bodies of nerve cells, and is responsible for the nervous system's higher functions. The cortex in turn has four major regions, called lobes; the frontal lobe handles movement, from planning movements to sending commands to the spinal cord. The parietal lobe processes somatosensation, the body's general senses such as touch; the occipital lobe is where the processing of vision begins; and the temporal lobe holds the area for hearing and regions crucial for forming memories. The diencephalon contains the thalamus, which relays information between the cerebrum and the rest of the nervous system, and the hypothalamus, which coordinates homeostasis. The brain stem, the part of the brain that connects with the spinal cord, controls basic functions such as breathing, heart rate and swallowing. The cerebellum, at the base of the brain on top of the brain stem, controls balance and helps with coordinating movement and learning new movement skills.
