# Schwann cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** other: sheath wrapped around a single axon segment  
  “Oligodendrocytes have processes that reach out to multiple axon segments, whereas the entire Schwann cell surrounds just one axon segment.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 12.2 Nervous Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue)
- **Nucleus position:** peripheral  
  “For Schwann cells, the outermost layer of the cell membrane contains cytoplasm and the nucleus of the cell as a bulge on one side of the myelin sheath.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 12.2 Nervous Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue)
- **Special structure:** myelin sheath  
  “Outside the plasma membrane of the axon is the myelin sheath, which is composed of the tightly wrapped plasma membrane of a Schwann cell.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 12.2 Nervous Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue)
- **Special structure:** myelin layers: the cell wraps around the axon several times, with little to no cytoplasm between the layers  
  “The glial cell is wrapped around the axon several times with little to no cytoplasm between the glial cell layers.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 12.2 Nervous Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue)
- **Special structure:** outer bulge on one side of the myelin sheath holding the cytoplasm and nucleus  
  “For Schwann cells, the outermost layer of the cell membrane contains cytoplasm and the nucleus of the cell as a bulge on one side of the myelin sheath.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 12.2 Nervous Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue)
- **Structure and job:** The Schwann cell insulates axons in the peripheral nervous system by covering them with myelin.  
  “The second type of glial cell is the Schwann cell, which insulate axons with myelin in the periphery.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 12.2 Nervous Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue)
- **Structure and job:** Because the entire Schwann cell surrounds the axon, each Schwann cell provides myelin for only one axon.  
  “This is distinctive from the PNS where a single Schwann cell provides myelin for only one axon as the entire Schwann cell surrounds the axon.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 35.1 Neurons and Glial Cells, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/35-1-neurons-and-glial-cells)
- **Structure and job:** Schwann cells wrap axons of the peripheral nervous system, either myelinating them or simply ensheathing them.  
  “A glial cell that myelinates or ensheathes axons in the peripheral nervous system.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0002573 Schwann cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0002573)
- **Structure and job:** Its myelin is a lipid-rich sheath around the axon that helps electrical signals travel along the axon.  
  “Myelin is a lipid-rich sheath that surrounds the axon and by doing so creates a myelin sheath that facilitates the transmission of electrical signals along the axon.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 12.2 Nervous Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue)
