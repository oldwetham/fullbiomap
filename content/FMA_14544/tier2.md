The rectum's main job is to store feces, the semisolid waste of digestion, until they leave the body. Waste leaving the sigmoid colon, the last part of the colon, enters the rectum inside the pelvis. The rectum has a thick muscular layer and continues to the anal canal, the final segment of the large intestine. Although the name rectum is Latin for “straight,” the rectum curves along the sacrum, the bone that forms the back of the pelvis. Connective tissue, the tissue that holds organs in place, firmly attaches the rectum to the sacrum. Three sideways bends in the rectum form internal folds called rectal valves, which help keep feces and gas from passing at the same time. Defecation, the removal of feces from the body, begins when movements of the colon force feces into the rectum and stretch the rectal wall. This push also sets off the defecation reflex, which clears feces from the rectum. The defecation reflex makes the sigmoid colon and the rectum contract. If a person delays defecation, the reflex contractions stop within a few seconds and the rectal walls relax.
