# Cardiac pacemaker cell of sinoatrial node: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Special structure:** gap junctions  
  “Because they are connected with gap junctions to surrounding muscle fibers and the specialized fibers of the heart’s conduction system, the pacemaker cells are able to transfer the depolarization to the other cardiac muscle fibers” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
- **Special structure:** sodium ion channels (slow Na+ influx)  
  “Conductive cells contain a series of sodium ion channels that allow a normal and slow influx of sodium ions that causes the membrane potential to rise slowly from an initial value of −60 mV up to about –40 mV.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 19.2 Cardiac Muscle and Electrical Activity, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-2-cardiac-muscle-and-electrical-activity)
- **Special structure:** calcium ion channels (rapid depolarization after the prepotential)  
  “The resulting movement of sodium ions creates spontaneous depolarization (or prepotential depolarization). At this point, calcium ion channels open and Ca2+ enters the cell, further depolarizing it at a more rapid rate until it reaches a value of approximately +15 mV.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 19.2 Cardiac Muscle and Electrical Activity, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-2-cardiac-muscle-and-electrical-activity)
- **Structure and job:** Being self-excitable, it depolarizes to threshold and fires action potentials on its own at set intervals, and those intervals set the heart rate.  
  “begins with the pacemaker cells. This group of cells is self-excitable and able to depolarize to threshold and fire action potentials on their own, a feature called autorhythmicity; they do this at set intervals which determine heart rate.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
- **Structure and job:** Gap junctions couple it to surrounding muscle fibers and conduction-system fibers, so it can pass its depolarization on to other cardiac muscle fibers and the heart contracts in a coordinated way.  
  “connected with gap junctions to surrounding muscle fibers and the specialized fibers of the heart’s conduction system, the pacemaker cells are able to transfer the depolarization to the other cardiac muscle fibers in a manner that allows the heart to contract in a coordinated manner.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
- **Structure and job:** Its sodium channels let sodium ions in slowly, so its membrane potential rises from about −60 mV to about −40 mV; this is its spontaneous, or prepotential, depolarization.  
  “sodium ion channels that allow a normal and slow influx of sodium ions that causes the membrane potential to rise slowly from an initial value of −60 mV up to about –40 mV. The resulting movement of sodium ions creates spontaneous depolarization (or prepotential depolarization).” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 19.2 Cardiac Muscle and Electrical Activity, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-2-cardiac-muscle-and-electrical-activity)
- **Structure and job:** Reaching threshold faster than any other part of the conduction system, it sets the pace, starting the impulses that spread to the other conducting cells.  
  “Since the SA node is the pacemaker, it reaches threshold faster than any other component of the conduction system. It will initiate the impulses spreading to the other conducting cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 19.2 Cardiac Muscle and Electrical Activity, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-2-cardiac-muscle-and-electrical-activity)
- **Structure and job:** It responds to autonomic nerve signals that speed up or slow down the heart rate, and to hormones that modulate heart rate to control blood pressure.  
  “Although cardiac muscle cannot be consciously controlled, the pacemaker cells respond to signals from the autonomic nervous system (ANS) to speed up or slow down the heart rate. The pacemaker cells can also respond to various hormones that modulate heart rate to control blood pressure.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
