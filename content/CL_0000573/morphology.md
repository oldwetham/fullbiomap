# Retinal cone cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Special structure:** inner segment and outer segment: the two parts of the cell  
  “Photoreceptor cells have two parts, the inner segment and the outer segment” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** inner segment, holding the nucleus and the common organelles  
  “The inner segment contains the nucleus and other common organelles of a cell” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** outer segment, the specialized region where photoreception takes place  
  “whereas the outer segment is a specialized region in which photoreception takes place” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** outer-segment infoldings of the cell membrane that hold the photopigments  
  “The cone-shaped outer segments of the cone photoreceptor contain their photosensitive pigments in infoldings of the cell membrane.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** outer-segment invaginations of the cell membrane that hold the photopigment  
  “In cones the photopigment is in invaginations of the cell membrane of the outer segment.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000573 retinal cone cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000573)
- **Special structure:** cone opsins: three photopigments, each sensitive to a particular wavelength  
  “There are three cone photopigments, called opsins, which are each sensitive to a particular wavelength of light.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** cone opsin peak sensitivities of 564, 534 and 420 nm (roughly red, green and blue)  
  “The three color opsins have peak sensitivities of 564 nm, 534 nm, and 420 nm corresponding roughly to the primary colors of red, green, and blue” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** three cone types with different photopsins: S (420 nm), M (530 nm) and L (560 nm) cones  
  “Some cones are maximally responsive to short light waves of 420 nm, so they are called S cones (“S” for “short”); others respond maximally to waves of 530 nm (M cones, for “medium”); a third group responds maximally to light of longer wavelengths, at 560 nm (L, or “long” cones).” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.5 Vision, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-5-vision)
- **Special structure:** opsin: a transmembrane protein carrying retinal, a vitamin A-related cofactor  
  “Opsin pigments are actually transmembrane proteins that contain a cofactor known as retinal. Retinal is a hydrocarbon molecule related to vitamin A.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** synaptic output: releases neurotransmitter onto bipolar cells in the outer synaptic layer  
  “The change in membrane potential alters the amount of neurotransmitter that the photoreceptor cells release onto bipolar cells in the outer synaptic layer.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** The cell splits its work in two: the inner segment holds the nucleus and everyday organelles, while the outer segment is specialized to capture light.  
  “The inner segment contains the nucleus and other common organelles of a cell, whereas the outer segment is a specialized region in which photoreception takes place.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Each of the three cone types carries an opsin tuned to different wavelengths, and the brain compares their activity to work out color.  
  “The three types of cone opsins, being sensitive to different wavelengths of light, provide us with color vision. By comparing the activity of the three different cones, the brain can extract color information from visual stimuli.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Cone opsins absorb light far less sensitively than rod rhodopsin, so cones serve vision in bright conditions while rods handle low light.  
  “The absorbance of rhodopsin in the rods is much more sensitive than in the cone opsins; specifically, rods are sensitive to vision in low light conditions, and cones are sensitive to brighter conditions.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Cones are only weakly light-sensitive and sit near the center of the retina; they respond to bright light and mainly serve daytime color vision.  
  “Cones are weakly photosensitive and are located near the center of the retina. They respond to bright light, and their primary role is in daytime, color vision.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.5 Vision, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-5-vision)
- **Structure and job:** Cones are packed densely into the fovea, the central spot of the retina responsible for acute vision.  
  “The fovea is the region in the center back of the eye that is responsible for acute vision. The fovea has a high density of cones.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.5 Vision, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-5-vision)
- **Structure and job:** Although less light-sensitive than rods, cones give vision finer spatial and temporal detail, and combining signals from cones with different pigments gives color vision.  
  “Cones are less sensitive to light than rods, but they provide vision with higher spatial and temporal acuity, and the combination of signals from cones with different pigments allows color vision.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000573 retinal cone cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000573)
- **Structure and job:** Light-activated opsin switches on a G protein that shifts the cell's membrane potential, so the cell releases less neurotransmitter into the outer synaptic layer.  
  “Activation of retinal and the opsin proteins result in activation of a G protein. The G protein changes the membrane potential of the photoreceptor cell, which then releases less neurotransmitter into the outer synaptic layer of the retina.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** In light the cone hyperpolarizes, which lifts its inhibition of bipolar cells.  
  “Exposure of the retina to light hyperpolarizes the rods and cones and removes their inhibition of bipolar cells.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.5 Vision, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-5-vision)
