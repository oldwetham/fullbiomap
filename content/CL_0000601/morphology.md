# Cochlear outer hair cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Surface:** stereocilia  
  “The longer stereocilia that project from the outer hair cells actually attach to the tectorial membrane.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Surface:** stereocilia  
  “The organs of Corti contain hair cells, which are named for the hair-like stereocilia extending from the cell’s apical surfaces” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** stereocilia array graded in height from tallest to shortest  
  “The stereocilia are an array of microvilli-like structures arranged from tallest to shortest.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** protein-fiber tethers linking adjacent stereocilia  
  “Protein fibers tether adjacent hairs together within each array, such that the array will bend in response to movements of the basilar membrane.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** About 12,000 outer hair cells, set in three or four rows, work to fine-tune incoming sound waves.  
  “The outer hair cells are arranged in three or four rows. They number approximately 12,000, and they function to fine tune incoming sound waves.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** Signals sent back from the brain make outer hair cells change length, which sharpens or dampens the hair cells' response to certain frequencies.  
  “The brain can send signals back to the cochlea, resulting in a change of length in the outer hair cells, sharpening or dampening the hair cells’ response to certain frequencies.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** Outer hair cells are motile, and this movement may actively help tune the cochlea's sensitivity and frequency selectivity.  
  “The motile properties of the outer hair cells may contribute actively to tuning the sensitivity and frequency selectivity of the cochlea.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000601 cochlear outer hair cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000601)
- **Structure and job:** The outer hair cell is a mechanoreceptor of the organ of Corti; in mammals these cells form three rows lying farther from the modiolus than the single row of inner hair cells.  
  “A mechanoreceptor in the organ of Corti. In mammals the outer hair cells are arranged in three rows which are further from the modiolus than the single row of inner hair cells.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000601 cochlear outer hair cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000601)
- **Structure and job:** Bending the stereocilia toward the tallest member of the array puts tension on the protein tethers, which opens ion channels in the hair cell membrane.  
  “When the stereocilia bend toward the tallest member of their array, tension in the protein tethers opens ion channels in the hair cell membrane.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** When its hairs bend, the hair cell releases an excitatory neurotransmitter at a synapse with a sensory neuron, which then carries action potentials to the central nervous system.  
  “When the hairs bend, they release an excitatory neurotransmitter at a synapse with a sensory neuron, which then conducts action potentials to the central nervous system.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** Outer hair cells are wired to only 10 percent of the afferent neurons, and each of those afferent neurons innervates many hair cells.  
  “Outer hair cells connect to only 10 percent of the afferent neurons, and each afferent neuron innervates many hair cells.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** Each hair cell along the cochlear duct is sensitive to a particular frequency, so together the hair cells let the cochlea separate sounds by frequency.  
  “The hair cells along the length of the cochlear duct, which are each sensitive to a particular frequency, allow the cochlea to separate auditory stimuli by frequency, just as a prism separates visible light into its component colors.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
