A non-myelinating Schwann cell wraps around several small-diameter axons, the long extensions of nerve cells that conduct nerve impulses. As its name says, it does not wrap these axons in myelin, a fatty insulating layer that surrounds many other axons and speeds up electrical signals. The non-myelinating Schwann cell is a glial cell, one of the cells that support nerve cells. It works in the peripheral nervous system, which is every part of the nervous system except the brain and spinal cord. Each non-myelinating Schwann cell is tucked in among the axons, separated from the nerve cell membranes by only tiny spaces. It also has a basal lamina, a thin layer outside the cell. A non-myelinating Schwann cell can survive even when no axon is present.
