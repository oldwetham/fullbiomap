Smooth muscle cells contract without conscious control to move food and to regulate blood flow in arteries. These cells are found in the walls of hollow organs, such as the stomach, and of blood vessels. Each cell is wide in the middle and tapered at both ends, like a football, and has one nucleus. Smooth muscle gets its name because its cells lack striations, a pattern of bands or stripes.
