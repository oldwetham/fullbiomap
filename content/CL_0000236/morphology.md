# B cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Nucleus position:** central  
  “The two basic types of lymphocytes, B cells and T cells, are identical morphologically with a large central nucleus surrounded by a thin layer of cytoplasm.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.1 Anatomy of the Lymphatic and Immune Systems, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-1-anatomy-of-the-lymphatic-and-immune-systems)
- **Nucleus size:** large (little cytoplasm)  
  “The two basic types of lymphocytes, B cells and T cells, are identical morphologically with a large central nucleus surrounded by a thin layer of cytoplasm.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.1 Anatomy of the Lymphatic and Immune Systems, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-1-anatomy-of-the-lymphatic-and-immune-systems)
- **Special structure:** B cell receptors: thousands of membrane-bound immunoglobulin (antibody) molecules coating the surface of the naive cell  
  “Similar to T cells, naïve B cells initially are coated in thousands of B cell receptors (BCRs), which are membrane-bound forms of Ig (immunoglobulin, or an antibody).” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.2 Adaptive Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-2-adaptive-immune-response)
- **Special structure:** B cell receptor built from two heavy chains and two light chains joined by disulfide linkages  
  “The B cell receptor has two heavy chains and two light chains connected by disulfide linkages.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.2 Adaptive Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-2-adaptive-immune-response)
- **Special structure:** surface IgM and IgD (both receptor classes on mature B cells leaving the bone marrow, with the same antigen specificity)  
  “Mature B cells that leave the bone marrow express both IgM and IgD, but both antibodies have the same antigen specificity.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.4 The Adaptive Immune Response: B-lymphocytes and Antibodies, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-4-the-adaptive-immune-response-b-lymphocytes-and-antibodies)
- **Special structure:** Ig alpha and Ig beta: two further membrane proteins, involved in signaling  
  “Two other membrane proteins, Ig alpha and Ig beta, are involved in signaling.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.2 Adaptive Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-2-adaptive-immune-response)
- **Special structure:** MHC class II molecules that display processed antigen to Th2 helper T cells  
  “They bind and engulf foreign antigens via their BCRs and then display processed antigens in the context of MHC II molecules to TH2 cells.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.2 Adaptive Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-2-adaptive-immune-response)
- **Structure and job:** Unlike a T cell, a B cell recognizes native, unprocessed antigen without needing MHC molecules or antigen-presenting cells.  
  “B cells do not recognize antigen in the complex fashion of T cells. B cells can recognize native, unprocessed antigen and do not require the participation of MHC molecules and antigen-presenting cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.4 The Adaptive Immune Response: B-lymphocytes and Antibodies, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-4-the-adaptive-immune-response-b-lymphocytes-and-antibodies)
- **Structure and job:** A B cell binds intact antigen because the antibodies it secretes must recognize the pathogen itself rather than digested remnants of it.  
  “B cells must be able to bind intact antigens because they secrete antibodies that must recognize the pathogen directly, rather than digested remnants of the pathogen.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.2 Adaptive Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-2-adaptive-immune-response)
- **Structure and job:** Each surface antibody has two binding sites, so an antigen with repeated units can crosslink the receptors, which is enough to activate the B cell without T cell cytokines.  
  “Each antibody on the B cell surface has two binding sites, and the repeated nature of T cell-independent antigen leads to crosslinking of the surface antibodies on the B cell. The crosslinking is enough to activate it in the absence of T cell cytokines.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.4 The Adaptive Immune Response: B-lymphocytes and Antibodies, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-4-the-adaptive-immune-response-b-lymphocytes-and-antibodies)
- **Structure and job:** It internalizes antigen caught by its surface antibody and presents processed pieces on class II MHC to Th2 helper cells, whose cytokines then complete its activation.  
  “Some of this antigen is internalized, processed, and presented to the Th2 cells on a class II MHC molecule. The T cell then binds using its antigen receptor and is activated to secrete cytokines that diffuse to the B cell, finally activating it completely.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.4 The Adaptive Immune Response: B-lymphocytes and Antibodies, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-4-the-adaptive-immune-response-b-lymphocytes-and-antibodies)
- **Structure and job:** All the receptors on one B cell are identical, but different B cells carry different recognition domains, so the population as a whole can bind a very wide range of molecular structures.  
  “The receptors of any particular B cell, as shown in are all the same, but the hundreds of millions of different B cells in an individual have distinct recognition domains that contribute to extensive diversity in the types of molecular structures to which they can bind.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.2 Adaptive Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-2-adaptive-immune-response)
