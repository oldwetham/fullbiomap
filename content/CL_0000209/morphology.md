# Taste receptor cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** other: elongated  
  “Each taste bud’s taste cells are replaced every 10 to 14 days. These are elongated cells with hair-like processes called microvilli at the tips that extend into the taste bud pore” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Surface:** microvilli  
  “Each taste bud’s taste cells are replaced every 10 to 14 days. These are elongated cells with hair-like processes called microvilli at the tips that extend into the taste bud pore” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Special structure:** taste receptors on the microvilli  
  “Food molecules (tastants) are dissolved in saliva, and they bind with and stimulate the receptors on the microvilli.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Structure and job:** Its microvilli reach into the taste bud pore, where food molecules dissolved in saliva bind to and stimulate the receptors on them.  
  “These are elongated cells with hair-like processes called microvilli at the tips that extend into the taste bud pore (illustrated in ). Food molecules (tastants) are dissolved in saliva, and they bind with and stimulate the receptors on the microvilli.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Structure and job:** Once activated by taste molecules, the cell signals onward by releasing neurotransmitter onto the dendrites of sensory neurons belonging to the facial, glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves.  
  “Once the gustatory cells are activated by the taste molecules, they release neurotransmitters onto the dendrites of sensory neurons. These neurons are part of the facial and glossopharyngeal cranial nerves, as well as a component within the vagus nerve dedicated to the gag reflex.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Sodium ions from salty food diffuse into the cell down their concentration gradient, depolarizing its membrane and generating a receptor potential.  
  “The Na+ concentration becomes high outside the gustatory cells, creating a strong concentration gradient that drives the diffusion of the ion into the cells. The entry of Na+ into these cells results in the depolarization of the cell membrane and the generation of a receptor potential.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Salty and sour tastes are triggered by Na+ and H+ ions, while the other tastes work through G protein–coupled receptors whose signalling depolarizes the cell.  
  “The first two tastes (salty and sour) are triggered by the cations Na+ and H+. The other tastes result from food molecules binding to a G protein–coupled receptor. A G protein signal transduction system ultimately leads to depolarization of the gustatory cell.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** As a sensory receptor cell, it converts a graded receptor potential in its membrane into neurotransmitter release at its synapses with sensory neurons.  
  “For other sensory receptor cells, such as taste cells or photoreceptors of the retina, graded potentials in their membranes result in the release of neurotransmitters at synapses with sensory neurons. This is called a receptor potential.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 12.5 Communication Between Neurons, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-5-communication-between-neurons)
