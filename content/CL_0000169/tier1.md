Beta cells make insulin, a hormone that lowers the amount of glucose, a simple sugar, in the blood. Beta cells make up about 75 percent of each pancreatic islet, a cluster of hormone-releasing cells in the pancreas. When blood glucose rises, such as after a meal, beta cells release insulin. The insulin signals muscle, fat and liver cells to take up the extra glucose from the blood.
