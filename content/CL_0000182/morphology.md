# Hepatocyte: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Special structure:** canaliculi: bile canaliculi run in grooves in the cell membranes between adjacent hepatocytes  
  “Between adjacent hepatocytes, grooves in the cell membranes provide room for each bile canaliculus (plural = canaliculi).” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.6 Accessory Organs in Digestion: The Liver, Pancreas, and Gallbladder, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-6-accessory-organs-in-digestion-the-liver-pancreas-and-gallbladder)
- **Organelles:** peroxisomes: 3 abundant  
  “The liver is the organ primarily responsible for detoxifying the blood before it travels throughout the body, and liver cells contain an exceptionally high number of peroxisomes.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 3.2 The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/3-2-the-cytoplasm-and-cellular-organelles)
- **Organelles:** rough ER: 3 abundant  
  “Since the RER is engaged in modifying proteins (such as enzymes, for example) that secrete from the cell, you would be correct in assuming that the RER is abundant in cells that secrete proteins. This is the case with liver cells, for example.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 4.4 The Endomembrane System and Proteins, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-4-the-endomembrane-system-and-proteins)
- **Structure and job:** As the liver’s main cell type, making up about 80 percent of its volume, hepatocytes take part in a wide variety of secretory, metabolic and endocrine functions.  
  “A hepatocyte is the liver’s main cell type, accounting for around 80 percent of the liver's volume. These cells play a role in a wide variety of secretory, metabolic, and endocrine functions.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.6 Accessory Organs in Digestion: The Liver, Pancreas, and Gallbladder, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-6-accessory-organs-in-digestion-the-liver-pancreas-and-gallbladder)
- **Structure and job:** Hepatocytes sit tightly packed around the fenestrated lining of the hepatic sinusoids, which gives them easy access to the blood whose nutrients, toxins and wastes they process.  
  “Hepatocytes are tightly packed around the fenestrated endothelium of these spaces, giving them easy access to the blood. From their central position, hepatocytes process the nutrients, toxins, and waste materials carried by the blood.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.6 Accessory Organs in Digestion: The Liver, Pancreas, and Gallbladder, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-6-accessory-organs-in-digestion-the-liver-pancreas-and-gallbladder)
- **Structure and job:** The bile canaliculi that run in grooves between neighboring hepatocytes are small ducts that collect the bile the cells produce.  
  “Between adjacent hepatocytes, grooves in the cell membranes provide room for each bile canaliculus (plural = canaliculi). These small ducts accumulate the bile produced by hepatocytes.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.6 Accessory Organs in Digestion: The Liver, Pancreas, and Gallbladder, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-6-accessory-organs-in-digestion-the-liver-pancreas-and-gallbladder)
- **Structure and job:** Hepatocytes are secretory cells that together release about one liter of bile each day.  
  “Hepatocytes secrete about one liter of bile each day.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.6 Accessory Organs in Digestion: The Liver, Pancreas, and Gallbladder, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-6-accessory-organs-in-digestion-the-liver-pancreas-and-gallbladder)
- **Structure and job:** Hepatocytes work in two directions: processed bilirubin is excreted into the bile canaliculi, while processed proteins, lipids and carbohydrates are secreted into the sinusoids or stored in the cell until needed.  
  “Materials such as bilirubin are processed and excreted into the bile canaliculi. Other materials including proteins, lipids, and carbohydrates are processed and secreted into the sinusoids or just stored in the cells until called upon.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.6 Accessory Organs in Digestion: The Liver, Pancreas, and Gallbladder, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-6-accessory-organs-in-digestion-the-liver-pancreas-and-gallbladder)
- **Structure and job:** Liver cells secrete proteins, and their abundant rough ER is where those proteins are modified before they leave the cell.  
  “Since the RER is engaged in modifying proteins (such as enzymes, for example) that secrete from the cell, you would be correct in assuming that the RER is abundant in cells that secrete proteins. This is the case with liver cells, for example.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 4.4 The Endomembrane System and Proteins, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-4-the-endomembrane-system-and-proteins)
- **Structure and job:** Liver cells carry an exceptionally high number of peroxisomes, fitting the liver’s role as the organ primarily responsible for detoxifying the blood before it travels through the body.  
  “The liver is the organ primarily responsible for detoxifying the blood before it travels throughout the body, and liver cells contain an exceptionally high number of peroxisomes.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 3.2 The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/3-2-the-cytoplasm-and-cellular-organelles)
- **Structure and job:** The peroxisomes inside liver cells are where alcohol is detoxified.  
  “For example, peroxisomes in liver cells detoxify alcohol.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 4.3 Eukaryotic Cells, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells)
- **Structure and job:** Hepatocytes either pass incoming glucose on into the circulation or store the excess as glycogen.  
  “In the liver, hepatocytes either pass the glucose on through the circulatory system or store excess glucose as glycogen.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 24.2 Carbohydrate Metabolism, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/24-2-carbohydrate-metabolism)
- **Structure and job:** Hepatocytes build fat from acetyl CoA (lipogenesis) in their cytoplasm.  
  “This process, called lipogenesis, creates lipids (fat) from the acetyl CoA and takes place in the cytoplasm of adipocytes (fat cells) and hepatocytes (liver cells).” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 24.3 Lipid Metabolism, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/24-3-lipid-metabolism)
- **Structure and job:** Hepatocytes strip the amine group from amino acids and convert most of the resulting toxic ammonia into urea.  
  “Ammonia (NH3) is a toxic byproduct of protein metabolism. It is formed as amino acids are deaminated by liver hepatocytes. That means that the amine group, NH2, is removed from amino acids as they are broken down. Most of the resulting ammonia is converted into urea by liver hepatocytes.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Structure and job:** Hepatocytes detoxify many of the toxins the body takes in from the environment or makes as byproducts of its own cell processes.  
  “Humans take in all sorts of toxins from the environment and also produce harmful chemicals as byproducts of cellular processes. Cells called hepatocytes in the liver detoxify many of these toxins.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 3.2 The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/3-2-the-cytoplasm-and-cellular-organelles)
