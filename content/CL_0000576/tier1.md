A monocyte is a white blood cell that gives rise to macrophages, cells that clean up dead and damaged cells. Monocytes circulate in the blood and in lymph, a watery fluid that bathes tissues and organs. When monocytes move into infected tissue, they turn into macrophages. Monocytes are usually easy to recognize by their large size and their indented or horseshoe-shaped nucleus.
