Paneth cells release lysozyme, a substance that kills bacteria, and can also surround and engulf particles, a process called phagocytosis. Paneth cells belong to the epithelium of the small intestine, the tissue that lines the intestine. Within this lining, Paneth cells sit in the intestinal glands, also called crypts of Lieberkühn. Between the villi, small hair-like projections of the lining, deep crevices lead into these tube-shaped glands. Lysozyme is an enzyme, a protein that speeds up chemical reactions, and it destroys bacteria by digesting the bacterial cell wall. Together with other substances, lysozyme kills most of the bacteria that enter the alimentary canal, the digestive tube from mouth to anus.
