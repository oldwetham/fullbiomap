B cells are immune cells that make antibodies, proteins that bind to bacteria, viruses and other foreign objects. A B cell works by turning into a plasma cell, a cell that releases antibodies. B cells mature in the bone marrow, the tissue inside bones where blood cells develop. B cells travel in the blood and live in the spleen and in lymph nodes, small bean-shaped organs.
