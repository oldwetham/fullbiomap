The urethra, the final passageway for urine, is a thin-walled tube that carries urine from the floor of the bladder to the outside of the body. The urethra begins at the trigone, a triangle-shaped area at the base of the bladder. From there, the urethra runs behind and below the pubic symphysis, the joint where the right and left hip bones meet. Two sphincters control the flow of urine through the urethra; a sphincter is a ring of muscle that can tighten to restrict flow. In females, the urethra is short, only 3 to 4 cm long, and opens to the outside just in front of the vaginal opening. In males, the urethra is much longer, about 20 cm, and carries both urine and semen, the fluid that contains sperm. The male urethra first passes through the prostate gland, which adds fluid to semen, and then through the floor of the pelvis into the penis. The longest part of the male urethra runs the whole length of the penis and opens to the outside at the tip of the penis. While reproductive fluids pass through the male urethra, sphincters squeeze tightly to keep urine out.
