Intestinal villus goblet cells secrete mucus, a thick, slippery substance that keeps tissues moist and acts as a lubricant. The goblet cells release this mucus, together with fluid, into the lumen, the open space inside the digestive tract. A goblet cell is a gland made of just one cell; a gland is a structure of one or more cells that make and release chemical substances. In the small intestine, goblet cells are found in the mucous membrane, a lining covered by protective mucus. Intestinal villus goblet cells are part of the cell layer covering each villus, a projection of the small intestine's lining. This covering layer is a simple columnar epithelium, a single layer of column-like cells, with goblet cells scattered through it.
