A pulmonary alveolar type 1 cell forms the thin barrier between air and blood that is essential for gas exchange. Type 1 cells cover almost all of the surface of the alveoli, the small, grape-like sacs in the lungs where gas exchange takes place. Each type 1 cell is large, flat and branched, and thin extensions of its cytoplasm, the material between the cell membrane and the nucleus, form the barrier. This layer is extremely thin and borders the lining of the capillaries, the smallest blood vessels. The alveolar and capillary walls together form the respiratory membrane, which gases cross by diffusion, moving from an area of higher concentration to one of lower concentration.
