- **Structure and job:** As keratinocytes, these cells store keratin, a fibrous intracellular protein that gives skin its hardness and water resistance.  
  “The cells in all of the layers except the stratum basale are called keratinocytes. A keratinocyte is a cell that manufactures and stores the protein keratin. Keratin is an intracellular fibrous protein that gives hair, nails, and skin their hardness and water-resistant properties.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** These dead cells are shed periodically and replaced by cells pushed up from deeper layers, so the whole layer is renewed in about 4 weeks.  
  “Cells in this layer are shed periodically and are replaced by cells pushed up from the stratum granulosum (or stratum lucidum in the case of the palms and soles of feet). The entire layer is replaced during a period of about 4 weeks.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
