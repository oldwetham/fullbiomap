Osteocytes are the main cells in mature bone, and they maintain its matrix, the nonliving material that fills the spaces between cells. Osteocytes keep up the mineral content of the matrix by releasing enzymes, which are proteins that speed up chemical reactions. Each osteocyte sits in a space called a lacuna, surrounded by bone tissue. A cross-section of long bone typically shows osteocytes in rings around a central canal, a channel that holds blood vessels and nerves. Long extensions from each osteocyte run through canaliculi, channels in the bone matrix, so osteocytes can communicate with each other and receive nutrients. The canaliculi connect with those of other lacunae and finally with the central canal, so nutrients reach the osteocytes and their wastes are removed. An osteocyte forms when an osteoblast, a bone-forming cell, becomes trapped in the surrounding matrix and changes in structure. Like osteoblasts, osteocytes cannot divide.
