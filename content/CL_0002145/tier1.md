Multiciliated cells help clear the airways of mucus, a sticky fluid that traps particles. These cells line the trachea, or windpipe, and the bronchi, its branches leading to the lungs. Each cell is column-shaped, with many cilia, short hair-like parts, on the side facing the airway. The cilia beat together, moving the mucus up to the throat to be swallowed.
