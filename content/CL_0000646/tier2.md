A basal cell is an unspecialized stem cell that divides to produce other kinds of epithelial cells, which cover and line the body. In the skin, basal cells are cube-shaped stem cells that give rise to cells of the epidermis, the skin's outermost layer. There, basal cells make up most of the stratum basale, the deepest layer of the epidermis. Basal cells in this layer keep dividing and produce all of the keratinocytes, the most common cells of the epidermis. As new cells form, older cells are pushed up and away from the stratum basale. In the hair bulb, at the base of the hair root, basal cells make the keratinocytes that start hair growth. Inside, a basal cell has little cytoplasm, the material between the cell membrane and the nucleus. The cell also has few organelles, the small structures that carry out a cell's work.
