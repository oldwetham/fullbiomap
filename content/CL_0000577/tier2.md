Type EC cells are one kind of enteroendocrine cell, a cell that releases hormones into the spaces between cells. Type EC cells release serotonin, which in the stomach acts as a hormone that makes stomach muscle contract. These cells also release enkephalins and substance P, which are neurotransmitters: chemicals that nerve endings release to act on target cells. Type EC cells are found in the gastrointestinal mucosa, the lining that forms the innermost layer of the digestive tract's wall. Within this lining, they occur especially in the glands of the duodenum and ileum, the first and last of the small intestine's three regions. In the stomach, Type EC cells occur especially in the glands of the pyloric antrum, the wider end of the pylorus, a funnel-shaped region that leads into the duodenum. Each Type EC cell holds secretory granules, small membrane-wrapped sacs that stain readily with silver (the argentaffin stain).
