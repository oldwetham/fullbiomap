Type II alveolar cells make surfactant, a detergent-like liquid in the airways. These cells sit in the walls of the alveoli, the small, grape-like sacs in the lungs. Surfactant mixes with the water lining the alveoli and helps lower the water's surface tension, a pull between water molecules. Without surfactant, the alveoli would collapse when you breathe out.
