The optic chiasm is where some nerve fibers from the eyes cross to the other side of the brain. This crossing lets each side of the brain handle the opposite half of what you see. The left and right optic nerves, the nerves of sight, meet at the optic chiasm. Found at the base of the brain, the optic chiasm takes its name from the Greek word for “crossing.”
