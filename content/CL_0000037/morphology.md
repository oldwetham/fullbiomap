# Hematopoietic stem cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Structure and job:** Living in adult bone marrow, it gives rise to red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets.  
  “Adult bone marrow has three distinct types of stem cells: hematopoietic stem cells, which give rise to red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 3.6 Cellular Differentiation, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/3-6-cellular-differentiation)
- **Structure and job:** Chemical signals called hemopoietic growth factors prompt it to divide and differentiate, which starts blood cell production.  
  “Hemopoiesis begins when the hematopoietic stem cell is exposed to appropriate chemical stimuli collectively called hemopoietic growth factors, which prompt it to divide and differentiate.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.2 Production of the Formed Elements, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-2-production-of-the-formed-elements)
- **Structure and job:** When it divides, one daughter cell stays a hematopoietic stem cell so blood cell production can continue, while the other becomes a more specialized stem cell.  
  “One daughter cell remains a hematopoietic stem cell, allowing hemopoiesis to continue. The other daughter cell becomes either of two types of more specialized stem cells” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.2 Production of the Formed Elements, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-2-production-of-the-formed-elements)
- **Structure and job:** In the red bone marrow it differentiates into the two blood cell lineages, myeloid and lymphoid.  
  “Hemopoiesis begins in the red bone marrow, with hematopoietic stem cells that differentiate into myeloid and lymphoid lineages.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.2 Production of the Formed Elements, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-2-production-of-the-formed-elements)
- **Structure and job:** Unlike embryonic stem cells, it persists throughout adulthood and continuously differentiates to replace blood cells lost to age or function.  
  “In contrast with embryonic stem cells, hematopoietic stem cells are present throughout adulthood and allow for the continuous differentiation of blood cells to replace those lost to age or function.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.1 Anatomy of the Lymphatic and Immune Systems, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-1-anatomy-of-the-lymphatic-and-immune-systems)
- **Structure and job:** Cytokines stimulate it to differentiate into the cells of the immune system.  
  “The cells of the immune system originate from hematopoietic stem cells in the bone marrow. Cytokines stimulate these stem cells to differentiate into immune cells.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.2 Adaptive Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-2-adaptive-immune-response)
- **Structure and job:** It also gives rise to mast cells, which, like blood cells, are part of the immune system.  
  “Like blood cells, mast cells are derived from hematopoietic stem cells and are part of the immune system.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.3 Connective Tissue Supports and Protects, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-3-connective-tissue-supports-and-protects)
