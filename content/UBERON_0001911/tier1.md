The mammary gland makes milk to feed newborns. Both sexes have mammary glands in the breast, but the glands usually work only in females. Tubes called lactiferous ducts carry the milk to the nipple. The hormone prolactin makes the gland produce milk, and the hormone oxytocin pushes the milk out.
