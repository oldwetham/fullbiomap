Outer hair cells fine-tune incoming sound waves, and about 12,000 of them form three or four rows. These cells sit in the cochlea, the hearing part of the inner ear. Each cell has stereocilia, hair-like projections that help detect sound waves. Brain signals can change the length of outer hair cells, sharpening or dampening responses to certain frequencies, or pitches.
