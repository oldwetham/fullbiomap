# Neutrophil: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** sphere  
  “A neutrophil is a phagocytic cell that is attracted via chemotaxis from the bloodstream to infected tissues. These spherical cells are granulocytes.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.2 Barrier Defenses and the Innate Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-2-barrier-defenses-and-the-innate-immune-response)
- **Size:** 10–12 µm diameter  
  “The most common of all the leukocytes, neutrophils will normally comprise 50–70 percent of total leukocyte count. They are 10–12 µm in diameter, significantly larger than erythrocytes.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Nuclei:** 1  
  “Neutrophils have a nucleus with two to five lobes, and they contain organelles, called lysosomes, that digest engulfed pathogens.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.1 Innate Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-1-innate-immune-response)
- **Nucleus shape:** multilobed (2–5 lobes)  
  “The nucleus has a distinct lobed appearance and may have two to five lobes, the number increasing with the age of the cell.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Special structure:** slender chromatin threads connecting the nuclear lobes  
  “a nucleus with three to five lobes connected by slender threads of chromatin” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000775 neutrophil, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000775)
- **Granules:** neutrophilic granules (show up most clearly with chemically neutral stains); look: quite fine, normally light lilac; abundance: many; position: unknown  
  “They are called neutrophils because their granules show up most clearly with stains that are chemically neutral (neither acidic nor basic). The granules are numerous but quite fine and normally appear light lilac.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** A rapid responder and efficient phagocyte that prefers bacteria, it carries lysozyme in its granules to break down bacterial cell walls.  
  “Neutrophils are rapid responders to the site of infection and are efficient phagocytes with a preference for bacteria. Their granules include lysozyme, an enzyme capable of lysing, or breaking down, bacterial cell walls” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** Its granules also carry oxidants such as hydrogen peroxide and defensins, proteins that pierce the membranes of bacteria and fungi so their contents leak out.  
  “Their granules include lysozyme, an enzyme capable of lysing, or breaking down, bacterial cell walls; oxidants such as hydrogen peroxide; and defensins, proteins that bind to and puncture bacterial and fungal plasma membranes, so that the cell contents leak out.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** Its lysosomes digest the pathogens it engulfs.  
  “A neutrophil is also a phagocytic leukocyte that engulfs and digests pathogens. Neutrophils, shown in , are the most abundant leukocytes of the immune system. Neutrophils have a nucleus with two to five lobes, and they contain organelles, called lysosomes, that digest engulfed pathogens.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.1 Innate Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-1-innate-immune-response)
- **Structure and job:** A spherical granulocyte and phagocyte, it is drawn by chemotaxis out of the bloodstream into infected tissue.  
  “A neutrophil is a phagocytic cell that is attracted via chemotaxis from the bloodstream to infected tissues. These spherical cells are granulocytes.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.2 Barrier Defenses and the Innate Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-2-barrier-defenses-and-the-innate-immune-response)
