Each superior oblique is an eye muscle that twists the eye around its own axis, helping the eyes focus on close objects. But the superior oblique also moves the eye down and away from the nose. This muscle starts at the back of the orbit, the bony socket that holds the eyeball. The muscle's tendon passes through the trochlea, a pulley made of cartilage, and attaches to the top of the eye.
