# Multiciliated columnar cell of tracheobronchial tree: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** column  
  “A multi-ciliated epithelial cell located in the trachea and bronchi, characterized by a columnar shape and motile cilia on its apical surface.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0002145 multiciliated columnar cell of tracheobronchial tree, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0002145)
- **Surface:** motile cilia  
  “A multi-ciliated epithelial cell located in the trachea and bronchi, characterized by a columnar shape and motile cilia on its apical surface.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0002145 multiciliated columnar cell of tracheobronchial tree, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0002145)
- **Surface:** motile cilia  
  “Cilia are found on many cells of the body, including the epithelial cells that line the airways of the respiratory system. Cilia move rhythmically; they beat constantly, moving waste materials such as dust, mucus, and bacteria upward through the airways, away from the lungs and toward the mouth.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 3.2 The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/3-2-the-cytoplasm-and-cellular-organelles)
- **Structure and job:** Its cilia move mucus and the particles trapped in it toward the pharynx, clearing the airway (mucociliary clearance).  
  “These cilia facilitate mucociliary clearance by moving mucus and trapped particles toward the pharynx.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0002145 multiciliated columnar cell of tracheobronchial tree, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0002145)
- **Structure and job:** With the rest of the ciliated airway lining it forms a mucociliary escalator, sweeping dust and pathogens trapped in mucus toward the throat.  
  “The ciliated epithelium of your airway forms a mucociliary escalator that sweeps particles of dust and pathogens trapped in the secreted mucus toward the throat.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.2 Epithelial Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-2-epithelial-tissue)
