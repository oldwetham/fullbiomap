The mouth takes in food and is the place where food begins to be broken down. Also called the oral cavity, the mouth is the first part of the digestive tract, a long tube that runs from the mouth to the anus. The lips, cheeks and palate form the borders of the mouth, with the palate as the roof. The lips and cheeks hold food in place for chewing, and they also help form words when you speak. At the back, an opening called the fauces leads from the mouth into the throat. The mouth is lined with stratified squamous epithelium, a tissue of several cell layers that protects surfaces from wear. The mouth holds the teeth and tongue and receives saliva, a digestive fluid made by the salivary glands. The teeth and tongue begin mechanical digestion, which makes food smaller without changing it chemically. Saliva begins chemical digestion, in which digestive fluids break complex food molecules into their chemical building blocks. To swallow, the tongue presses up and back against the palate, pushing the bolus, or mass of chewed food, to the back of the mouth.
