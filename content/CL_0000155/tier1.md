A peptic cell, or chief cell, makes pepsin, an enzyme that digests protein in the stomach. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up chemical reactions in the body. Peptic cells sit mostly near the base of the gastric glands, which release a digestive fluid called gastric juice. Peptic cells release pepsin as pepsinogen, an inactive form that needs hydrochloric acid to become pepsin.
