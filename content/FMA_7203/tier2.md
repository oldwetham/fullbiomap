Each kidney filters the blood, removes wastes and passes them out in urine; the kidneys are the main organs of the urinary system. The kidneys also help regulate blood pressure, carry out the last step in making active vitamin D, and make EPO, a hormone that triggers the bone marrow to produce red blood cells. Each kidney lies beside the spine, between the back wall of the abdomen and the peritoneum, the membrane that lines the abdominal and pelvic cavity. The kidney is bean-shaped, with a dent on its inner side, the hilum, where blood vessels and nerves enter and leave. Inside, an outer reddish region, the renal cortex, surrounds a darker inner region, the renal medulla, which is made of cone-shaped renal pyramids. The cortex and medulla hold over a million nephrons, the working units that carry out all the filtering and processing that produce urine. Each nephron starts with a glomerulus, a tuft of capillaries, and continues as a tube that sets the final makeup of the urine. The glomerulus filters the blood mainly by particle size, making a fluid called filtrate that lacks cells and large proteins. Ninety-nine percent of this filtrate is reabsorbed, or taken back into the blood, so only about 1 to 2 liters of urine are made each day. Urine collects in the renal pelvis, a large cavity at the center of the kidney that leads into the ureter, the tube that carries urine to the bladder.
