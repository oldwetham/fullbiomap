Pericytes help regrow smooth muscle, the muscle in the walls of hollow organs and around blood vessels. Thanks to pericytes, smooth muscle regrows and repairs much more readily than heart muscle or skeletal muscle, the muscle attached to bones. A pericyte is a type of stem cell, an unspecialized cell that can divide and become specialized cells. A pericyte may become a fibroblast (a cell that releases protein fibers) or a smooth muscle cell. A pericyte may also become a macrophage, a large cell that swallows up foreign particles. Pericytes are found in capillaries, the smallest blood vessels, where the wall lacks a true muscle layer. Pericytes also wrap around arterioles, the very small arteries that lead to capillaries. Around arterioles, each pericyte lies outside the basement membrane, a thin layer of fibrous material. Each pericyte is a long, contractile cell, meaning it can shorten with force.
