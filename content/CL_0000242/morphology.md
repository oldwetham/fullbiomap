# Merkel cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Structure and job:** Set among the basal cells of the stratum basale, the Merkel cell acts as a receptor that stimulates sensory nerves, and the brain perceives those signals as touch.  
  “Two other cell types are found dispersed among the basal cells in the stratum basale. The first is a Merkel cell, which functions as a receptor and is responsible for stimulating sensory nerves that the brain perceives as touch.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** Merkel cells, which act as touch receptors by stimulating sensory nerves, are especially abundant on the surfaces of the hands and feet.  
  “The first is a Merkel cell, which functions as a receptor and is responsible for stimulating sensory nerves that the brain perceives as touch. These cells are especially abundant on the surfaces of the hands and feet.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** Scattered through the stratum basale, Merkel cells serve as touch receptors.  
  “Merkel cells, seen scattered in the stratum basale, are also touch receptors.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.3 Functions of the Integumentary System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-3-functions-of-the-integumentary-system)
- **Structure and job:** Placed in the stratum basale of the epidermis, Merkel cells are type I cutaneous mechanoreceptors that sense the low-frequency vibrations the skin makes when a finger is dragged across a textured surface.  
  “If you drag your finger across a textured surface, the skin of your finger will vibrate. Such low frequency vibrations are sensed by mechanoreceptors called Merkel cells, also known as type I cutaneous mechanoreceptors. Merkel cells are located in the stratum basale of the epidermis.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
