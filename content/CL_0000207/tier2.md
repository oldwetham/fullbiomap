Olfactory receptor cells are neurons (cells that receive and send signals) that detect chemicals for the sense of smell. Olfactory receptor cells sit in the olfactory epithelium, a small patch of tissue high inside the nose. Each of these cells is a bipolar neuron, which means two extensions reach out from its cell body. Each cell has a single dendrite, a branch that takes in signals, buried in the olfactory epithelium. From this dendrite, 5 to 20 hair-like cilia stick out and trap odor molecules. The cilia of each cell carry only one type of receptor, a protein that binds a specific molecule. Because of this, each cell is specialized to detect specific odor molecules. When the cell binds certain inhaled molecules, it sends signals directly to the olfactory bulb, a brain structure that receives smell signals. Each cell reaches the olfactory bulb through a thin axon, a branch that carries signals away from the cell body. Olfactory receptor cells live only about one to four months, and new ones are made regularly.
