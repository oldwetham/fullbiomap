Kidney collecting duct intercalated cells help regulate blood pH, a measure of how acidic or basic the blood is. Intercalated cells are one of the two distinct cell types in the kidney's collecting ducts, the tubes that gather urine as it forms. While principal cells, the other type, recover or lose sodium and potassium, intercalated cells secrete or absorb acid or bicarbonate, a substance that lowers the acidity of a solution. Intercalated cells have pumps that actively push hydrogen ions, which make liquids more acidic, into the urine forming in the ducts, so that the ions leave the body. These pumps sit on the apical surfaces of intercalated cells, the sides that face the open space inside the ducts. Intercalated cells also take back potassium and bicarbonate while secreting hydrogen ions; together, these actions make the blood less acidic and the urine more acidic.
