# Endothelial cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Nucleus shape:** flattened  
  “Squamous cell nuclei tend to be flat, horizontal, and elliptical, mirroring the form of the cell. The endothelium is the epithelial tissue that lines vessels of the lymphatic and cardiovascular system, and it is made up of a single layer of squamous cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.2 Epithelial Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-2-epithelial-tissue)
- **Special structure:** tight junctions  
  “Continuous capillaries are characterized by a complete endothelial lining with tight junctions between endothelial cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.1 Structure and Function of Blood Vessels, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-1-structure-and-function-of-blood-vessels)
- **Special structure:** transport vesicles  
  “Some large proteins in blood plasma can move into and out of the endothelial cells packaged within vesicles by endocytosis and exocytosis.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.3 Capillary Exchange, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-3-capillary-exchange)
- **Special structure:** fenestrations (pores), in fenestrated capillaries  
  “A fenestrated capillary is one that has pores (or fenestrations) in addition to tight junctions in the endothelial lining.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.1 Structure and Function of Blood Vessels, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-1-structure-and-function-of-blood-vessels)
- **Special structure:** basement membrane (basal lamina)  
  “Next to the endothelium is the basement membrane, or basal lamina, that effectively binds the endothelium to the connective tissue.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.1 Structure and Function of Blood Vessels, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-1-structure-and-function-of-blood-vessels)
- **Structure and job:** Because each cell is so thin, simple squamous epithelium such as the capillary lining is found where chemical compounds must pass through rapidly.  
  “Simple squamous epithelium, because of the thinness of the cell, is present where rapid passage of chemical compounds is observed. The alveoli of lungs where gases diffuse, segments of kidney tubules, and the lining of capillaries are also made of simple squamous epithelial tissue.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.2 Epithelial Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-2-epithelial-tissue)
- **Structure and job:** In capillaries the tight junctions between endothelial cells are often incomplete, leaving intercellular clefts through which water and very small molecules pass between plasma and tissue fluid.  
  “Although a tight junction is usually impermeable and only allows for the passage of water and ions, they are often incomplete in capillaries, leaving intercellular clefts that allow for exchange of water and other very small molecules between the blood plasma and the interstitial fluid.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.1 Structure and Function of Blood Vessels, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-1-structure-and-function-of-blood-vessels)
- **Structure and job:** In capillaries this single layer of endothelial cells is where oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse to and from red blood cells, and where materials are exchanged by endocytosis and exocytosis.  
  “In capillaries, this single layer of cells is the location of diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide between the endothelial cells and red blood cells, as well as the exchange site via endocytosis and exocytosis.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.3 Mammalian Heart and Blood Vessels, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-3-mammalian-heart-and-blood-vessels)
- **Structure and job:** Pores (fenestrations) in the endothelial lining of fenestrated capillaries let larger molecules cross the capillary wall.  
  “A fenestrated capillary is one that has pores (or fenestrations) in addition to tight junctions in the endothelial lining. These make the capillary permeable to larger molecules.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.1 Structure and Function of Blood Vessels, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-1-structure-and-function-of-blood-vessels)
- **Structure and job:** Because the endothelium lacks thrombogenic tissue factor, clotting factors flowing past it stay in their inactive state.  
  “Clotting factors flow through the blood vessels in their inactive state. The endothelium does not have thrombogenic tissue factor to activate clotting factors.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.5 Hemostasis, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-5-hemostasis)
- **Structure and job:** Endothelial cells release endothelins, local chemicals that constrict the smooth muscle in the vessel wall and so raise blood pressure.  
  “The endothelium releases local chemicals called endothelins that can constrict the smooth muscle within the walls of the vessel to increase blood pressure.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.1 Structure and Function of Blood Vessels, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-1-structure-and-function-of-blood-vessels)
- **Structure and job:** When acetylcholine stimulates them, endothelial cells release nitric oxide, which dilates the vessel.  
  “Many of these are cholinergic neurons, that is, they release acetylcholine, which in turn stimulates the vessels’ endothelial cells to release nitric oxide (NO), which causes vasodilation.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.4 Homeostatic Regulation of the Vascular System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-4-homeostatic-regulation-of-the-vascular-system)
- **Structure and job:** In capillaries and venules, white blood cells leave the blood by adhering to the endothelial lining and squeezing through adjacent cells into the tissue fluid.  
  “Venules as well as capillaries are the primary sites of emigration or diapedesis, in which the white blood cells adhere to the endothelial lining of the vessels and then squeeze through adjacent cells to enter the tissue fluid.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.1 Structure and Function of Blood Vessels, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-1-structure-and-function-of-blood-vessels)
