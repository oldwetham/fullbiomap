# Primary oocyte: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Special structure:** zona pellucida: a thin acellular membrane the primary oocyte secretes within the growing secondary follicle  
  “Within the growing secondary follicle, the primary oocyte now secretes a thin acellular membrane called the zona pellucida that will play a critical role in fertilization.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.2 Anatomy and Physiology of the Ovarian Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-2-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-ovarian-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** A primary oocyte is defined by its stage: it is an oocyte that has not yet completed female meiosis I.  
  “A primary oocyte is an oocyte that has not completed female meosis I.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000654 primary oocyte, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000654)
- **Structure and job:** It begins the first meiotic division but halts in prophase I, and at birth every future egg is held at this stage.  
  “The cell starting meiosis is called a primary oocyte, as shown in . This cell will start the first meiotic division and be arrested in its progress in the first prophase stage. At the time of birth, all future eggs are in the prophase stage.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 43.3 Human Reproductive Anatomy and Gametogenesis, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/43-3-human-reproductive-anatomy-and-gametogenesis)
- **Structure and job:** Primary oocytes form in the fetal ovary before birth and stay paused in meiosis I for years, resuming it from puberty until near menopause.  
  “Unlike spermatogonia, however, oogonia form primary oocytes in the fetal ovary prior to birth. These primary oocytes are then arrested in this stage of meiosis I, only to resume it years later, beginning at puberty and continuing until the person is near menopause” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.2 Anatomy and Physiology of the Ovarian Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-2-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-ovarian-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** Enclosed in its follicle, the oocyte stays a primary oocyte through the primordial, primary, secondary and tertiary follicle stages until just before ovulation.  
  “follicles progress from primordial, to primary, to secondary and tertiary stages prior to ovulation—with the oocyte inside the follicle remaining as a primary oocyte until right before ovulation.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.2 Anatomy and Physiology of the Ovarian Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-2-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-ovarian-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** Just before ovulation, the surge of luteinizing hormone restarts its meiosis, turning the primary oocyte into a secondary oocyte.  
  “Just prior to ovulation, a surge of luteinizing hormone triggers the resumption of meiosis in a primary oocyte. This initiates the transition from primary to secondary oocyte.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.2 Anatomy and Physiology of the Ovarian Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-2-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-ovarian-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** Finishing meiosis I, it divides unequally: most of its cellular material and organelles go to the secondary oocyte, while the polar body gets only a set of chromosomes and a little cytoplasm.  
  “This results in the primary oocyte finishing the first meiotic division. The cell divides unequally, with most of the cellular material and organelles going to one cell, called a secondary oocyte, and only one set of chromosomes and a small amount of cytoplasm going to the other cell.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 43.3 Human Reproductive Anatomy and Gametogenesis, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/43-3-human-reproductive-anatomy-and-gametogenesis)
