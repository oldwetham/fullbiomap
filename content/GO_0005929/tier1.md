Cilia are short, hair-like structures that move substances along a cell's outer surface. In the airways, cilia beat constantly, sweeping dust, mucus and bacteria away from the lungs. In the fallopian tubes, beating cilia move egg cells from the ovary toward the uterus. Cilia are built from microtubules, which are small hollow tubes.
