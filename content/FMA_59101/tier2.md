Each lacrimal gland produces tears, which wash across the surface of the eye. The gland lies just inside the orbit, the bony socket of the eye, above and to the outer side of the eyeball. Each lacrimal gland is an almond-shaped exocrine gland that makes the watery part of tears; like other exocrine glands, it releases its contents through ducts. Tears keep the surface of the eye moist. Tears from the gland flow over the surface of the eyeball to the inner corner of the eye, washing away foreign particles. Tears also help defend the eyes, which skin does not protect, by trapping and rinsing away pathogens, agents such as microorganisms that cause disease. At the eye's inner corner, the tears drain into the nasolacrimal canal, which runs down into the nasal cavity, the inside of the nose. Nerve fibers in the facial nerve, the nerve that also controls the muscles of the face, influence tear production.
