# Pulmonary alveolar type 1 cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** other: flattened and branched squamous cell  
  “A squamous pulmonary alveolar epithelial cell that is flattened and branched.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0002062 pulmonary alveolar type 1 cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0002062)
- **Special structure:** thin cytoplasmic extensions (50–100 nm)  
  “This large cell has thin (50-100 nm) cytoplasmic extensions to form the air-blood barrier essential for normal gas exchange.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0002062 pulmonary alveolar type 1 cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0002062)
- **Special structure:** thin, elastic basement membrane that the epithelium is attached to  
  “The simple squamous epithelium formed by type I alveolar cells is attached to a thin, elastic basement membrane.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 22.1 Organs and Structures of the Respiratory System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-1-organs-and-structures-of-the-respiratory-system)
- **Structure and job:** This large cell covers more than 98% of the alveolar surface, and its thin (50–100 nm) cytoplasmic extensions form the air-blood barrier that normal gas exchange depends on.  
  “A pulmonary alveolar type 1 cell covers more than 98% of the alveolar surface. This large cell has thin (50-100 nm) cytoplasmic extensions to form the air-blood barrier essential for normal gas exchange.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0002062 pulmonary alveolar type 1 cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0002062)
- **Structure and job:** These squamous cells cover up to 97 percent of the alveolar surface yet are only about 25 nm thick, making a broad, very thin lining that is highly permeable to gases.  
  “A type I alveolar cell is a squamous epithelial cell of the alveoli, which constitute up to 97 percent of the alveolar surface area. These cells are about 25 nm thick and are highly permeable to gases.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 22.1 Organs and Structures of the Respiratory System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-1-organs-and-structures-of-the-respiratory-system)
- **Structure and job:** The extremely thin epithelium these cells form borders the capillary endothelium, and together they make a respiratory membrane about 0.5 μm thick that gases cross by simple diffusion.  
  “This epithelium is extremely thin and borders the endothelial membrane of capillaries. Taken together, the alveoli and capillary membranes form a respiratory membrane that is approximately 0.5 μm (micrometers) thick. The respiratory membrane allows gases to cross by simple diffusion” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 22.1 Organs and Structures of the Respiratory System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-1-organs-and-structures-of-the-respiratory-system)
