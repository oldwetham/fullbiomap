Each fallopian tube uses tiny, hair-like cilia to move eggs from the ovary toward the uterus. Each tube reaches from the uterus to the ovary but does not touch the ovary. Instead, finger-like projections called fimbriae help a released egg enter the tube. Fertilization, when a sperm joins an egg, usually happens inside the tube.
