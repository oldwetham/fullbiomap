# Sertoli cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** branched  
  “Surrounding all stages of the developing sperm cells are elongate, branching Sertoli cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Special structure:** tight junctions between neighboring Sertoli cells (the blood–testis barrier)  
  “Tight junctions between these sustentacular cells create the blood–testis barrier” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** Elongate and branching, Sertoli cells surround the developing sperm cells at every stage of their development.  
  “Surrounding all stages of the developing sperm cells are elongate, branching Sertoli cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** Each Sertoli cell reaches from the basement membrane at the edge of the seminiferous tubule all the way to the lumen, physically wrapping around the germ cells.  
  “They extend physically around the germ cells from the peripheral basement membrane of the seminiferous tubules to the lumen.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** Sertoli cells project inward from the basement membrane of the seminiferous tubules, where they surround and nourish the developing male germ cells.  
  “A supporting cell projecting inward from the basement membrane of seminiferous tubules. They surround and nourish the developing male germ cells” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000216 Sertoli cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000216)
- **Structure and job:** Sertoli cells secrete androgen-binding protein.  
  “They surround and nourish the developing male germ cells and secrete androgen binding protein.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000216 Sertoli cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000216)
- **Structure and job:** Tight junctions joining neighboring Sertoli cells form the blood–testis barrier, which keeps bloodborne substances away from the germ cells and keeps the germ cells' surface antigens out of the bloodstream, where they could trigger an autoimmune response.  
  “Tight junctions between these sustentacular cells create the blood–testis barrier, which keeps bloodborne substances from reaching the germ cells and, at the same time, keeps surface antigens on developing germ cells from escaping into the bloodstream and prompting an autoimmune response.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** Sertoli cells secrete signaling molecules that promote sperm production and can determine whether germ cells live or die.  
  “Sertoli cells secrete signaling molecules that promote sperm production and can control whether germ cells live or die.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** In the testis, follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) binds mainly to Sertoli cells in the seminiferous tubules, and this promotes sperm production.  
  “In the testes, FSH binds predominantly to the Sertoli cells within the seminiferous tubules to promote spermatogenesis.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** When sperm count is too high, Sertoli cells release the hormone inhibin into the blood, which reduces GnRH and FSH release and so slows sperm production.  
  “The Sertoli cells produce the hormone inhibin, which is released into the blood when the sperm count is too high. This inhibits the release of GnRH and FSH, which will cause spermatogenesis to slow down.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 43.4 Hormonal Control of Human Reproduction, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/43-4-hormonal-control-of-human-reproduction)
- **Structure and job:** Sertoli cells act as "nursemaid" cells mixed among the sperm cells, protecting the germ cells and promoting their development.  
  “The sperm cells are mixed with “nursemaid” cells called Sertoli cells which protect the germ cells and promote their development.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 43.3 Human Reproductive Anatomy and Gametogenesis, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/43-3-human-reproductive-anatomy-and-gametogenesis)
