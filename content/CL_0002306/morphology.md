- **Structure and job:** Because it actively transports ions across its membranes, it is packed with mitochondria that make enough ATP to power that work.  
  “These cells actively transport ions across their membranes, so they possess a high concentration of mitochondria in order to produce sufficient ATP.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.4 Microscopic Anatomy of the Kidney, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-4-microscopic-anatomy-of-the-kidney)
- **Structure and job:** More substances cross its membranes than anywhere else in the nephron, with amino acids and glucose moved by symporters together with Na+.  
  “More substances move across the membranes of the PCT than any other portion of the nephron. Many of these substances (amino acids and glucose) use symport mechanisms for transport along with Na+.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Structure and job:** Na+/K+ ATPase pumps on its basal surface move Na+ out of the cell, supplying the energy that drives glucose transport through it.  
  “The energy to move glucose comes from the Na+/K+ ATPase that pumps Na+ out of the cell on the basal surface.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Structure and job:** It reclaims about 67 percent of the water, Na+ and K+ entering the nephron and almost all of the filtered glucose, amino acids and vitamins.  
  “About 67 percent of the water, Na+, and K+ entering the nephron is reabsorbed in the PCT and returned to the circulation. Almost 100 percent of glucose, amino acids, and other organic substances such as vitamins are normally recovered here.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Structure and job:** Aquaporin water channels in the PCT let water be reabsorbed into the peritubular capillaries.  
  “Water is also independently reabsorbed into the peritubular capillaries due to the presence of aquaporins, or water channels, in the PCT.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 41.2 The Kidneys and Osmoregulatory Organs, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/41-2-the-kidneys-and-osmoregulatory-organs)
- **Structure and job:** A Na+/H+ antiporter releases H+ into the lumen while taking back Na+, recycling hydrogen ions so that bicarbonate can be recovered.  
  “At the same time this is occurring, a Na+/H+ antiporter excretes H+ into the lumen, while it recovers Na+. Note how the hydrogen ion is recycled so that bicarbonate can be recovered.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Structure and job:** By actively secreting H+ into the forming urine as Na+ is reabsorbed, these cells help the body get rid of excess acid and raise blood pH.  
  “The cells of the PCT actively secrete H+ into the forming urine as Na+ is reabsorbed. The body rids itself of excess H+ and raises blood pH.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.9 Regulation of Fluid Volume and Composition, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-9-regulation-of-fluid-volume-and-composition)
- **Structure and job:** The PCT is a target of parathyroid hormone, which stimulates the conversion of calcidiol into calcitriol, the active form of vitamin D, there.  
  “Parathyroid hormone (PTH) is an 84-amino acid peptide produced by the parathyroid glands in response to decreased circulating Ca++ levels. Among its targets is the PCT, where it stimulates the hydroxylation of calcidiol to calcitriol (1,25-hydroxycholecalciferol, the active form of vitamin D).” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.8 Endocrine Regulation of Kidney Function, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-8-endocrine-regulation-of-kidney-function)
