# Basophil: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Size:** 8–10 µm diameter  
  “They are slightly smaller than neutrophils and eosinophils at 8–10 µm in diameter.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Nuclei:** 1  
  “in its mature form has an irregularly shaped, pale-staining nucleus that is partially constricted into two lobes” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000767 basophil, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000767)
- **Nucleus shape:** bilobed  
  “Basophils contain large granules that pick up a dark blue stain and are so common they may make it difficult to see the two-lobed nucleus.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Nucleus shape:** bilobed  
  “an irregularly shaped, pale-staining nucleus that is partially constricted into two lobes” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000767 basophil, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000767)
- **Granules:** granules that stain best with basic (alkaline) stains; look: large, dark blue; abundance: many; position: unknown  
  “The granules of basophils stain best with basic (alkaline) stains. Basophils contain large granules that pick up a dark blue stain and are so common they may make it difficult to see the two-lobed nucleus.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Special structure:** histamine (released by the granules)  
  “The granules of basophils release histamines, which contribute to inflammation, and heparin, which opposes blood clotting.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Special structure:** heparin (released by the granules)  
  “The granules of basophils release histamines, which contribute to inflammation, and heparin, which opposes blood clotting.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Special structure:** serotonin (a vasoactive amine the cell contains)  
  “Basophils contain vasoactive amines such as histamine and serotonin, which are released on appropriate stimulation.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000767 basophil, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000767)
- **Structure and job:** Its granules release histamine, which promotes inflammation, and heparin, which opposes blood clotting.  
  “The granules of basophils release histamines, which contribute to inflammation, and heparin, which opposes blood clotting.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** It holds vasoactive amines such as histamine and serotonin and releases them when it is appropriately stimulated.  
  “Basophils contain vasoactive amines such as histamine and serotonin, which are released on appropriate stimulation.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000767 basophil, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000767)
- **Structure and job:** Its release of histamine is often part of hypersensitive immune responses to harmless antigens such as pollen.  
  “A hypersensitive immune response to harmless antigens, such as in pollen, often involves the release of histamine by basophils and mast cells.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.1 Innate Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-1-innate-immune-response)
