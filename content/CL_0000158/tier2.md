Club cells help protect the lining of the tracheobronchial tree, the system of air tubes that runs from the windpipe to the air sacs of the lungs. A club cell has no cilia, the short, hair-like structures that move substances along a cell's outer surface. Instead, a club cell has short microvilli, which are thin, membrane-covered projections on a cell's surface. Club cells can multiply, and they can also differentiate into ciliated cells, a process in which cells become specialized in structure and function. In this way, club cells rebuild the lining of the bronchioles, the smaller and smaller air tubes that spread through the lungs.
