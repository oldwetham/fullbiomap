# Cardiac muscle cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** branched  
  “Cardiac muscle cells branch freely.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 19.2 Cardiac Muscle and Electrical Activity, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-2-cardiac-muscle-and-electrical-activity)
- **Nuclei:** 1  
  “cardiac muscle fibers are shorter than skeletal muscle fibers and usually contain only one nucleus” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
- **Nucleus position:** central  
  “Unlike skeletal muscle fibers, cardiomyocytes are single cells typically with a single centrally located nucleus.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.4 Muscle Tissue and Motion, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-4-muscle-tissue-and-motion)
- **Organelles:** mitochondria: 3 abundant  
  “Cardiac muscle fibers also possess many mitochondria and myoglobin, as ATP is produced primarily through aerobic metabolism.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
- **Special structure:** intercalated discs (joining the cells at their ends)  
  “Cardiac muscle fibers cells also are extensively branched and are connected to one another at their ends by intercalated discs.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
- **Special structure:** gap junctions (in the intercalated discs)  
  “Intercalated discs have both anchoring junctions and gap junctions.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.4 Muscle Tissue and Motion, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-4-muscle-tissue-and-motion)
- **Special structure:** desmosomes (in the intercalated discs)  
  “The remainder of the intercalated disc is composed of desmosomes.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
- **Special structure:** T tubules (only at the Z discs)  
  “The T tubules are only found at the Z discs, whereas in skeletal muscle, they are found at the junction of the A and I bands.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 19.2 Cardiac Muscle and Electrical Activity, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-2-cardiac-muscle-and-electrical-activity)
- **Special structure:** striations (sarcomeres along the length of the cell)  
  “Cardiac muscle also demonstrates striations, the alternating pattern of dark A bands and light I bands attributed to the precise arrangement of the myofilaments and fibrils that are organized in sarcomeres along the length of the cell” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 19.2 Cardiac Muscle and Electrical Activity, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-2-cardiac-muscle-and-electrical-activity)
- **Special structure:** myoglobin  
  “Cardiac muscle fibers also possess many mitochondria and myoglobin, as ATP is produced primarily through aerobic metabolism.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
- **Structure and job:** Intercalated discs let the cells contract in a wave, so the heart can work as a pump.  
  “An intercalated disc allows the cardiac muscle cells to contract in a wave-like pattern so that the heart can work as a pump.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)
