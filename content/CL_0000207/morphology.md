# Olfactory receptor cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** other: bipolar neuron (two processes extending from the cell body)  
  “Olfactory neurons are bipolar neurons (neurons with two processes extending from the cell body).” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Special structure:** single dendrite, buried in the olfactory epithelium  
  “Each neuron has a single dendrite buried in the olfactory epithelium” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Special structure:** olfactory cilia: 5–20 hair-like, receptor-laden cilia extending from the dendrite  
  “extending from this dendrite are 5 to 20 receptor-laden, hair-like cilia that trap odorant molecules” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Special structure:** odorant receptor proteins on the cilia  
  “The sensory receptors on the cilia are proteins, and it is the variations in their amino acid chains that make the receptors sensitive to different odorants.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Special structure:** G protein–coupled odorant receptors in the cell membrane of the dendrite  
  “The odorant–protein complex binds to a receptor protein within the cell membrane of an olfactory dendrite. These receptors are G protein–coupled, and will produce a graded membrane potential in the olfactory neurons.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** thin, unmyelinated axon (no myelin sheath)  
  “Olfactory neurons project from the olfactory epithelium to the olfactory bulb as thin, unmyelinated axons.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Structure and job:** As a neuron that can detect chemical stimuli, it takes part in the sense of smell.  
  “Any neuron that is capable of some detection of chemical stimulus involved in sensory perception of smell.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000207 olfactory receptor cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000207)
- **Structure and job:** Each neuron carries only one type of receptor on its cilia, so each neuron is specialized to detect specific odorants.  
  “Each olfactory sensory neuron has only one type of receptor on its cilia, and the receptors are specialized to detect specific odorants, so the bipolar neurons themselves are specialized.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Structure and job:** When its dendrite binds inhaled molecules, the neuron sends impulses directly to the olfactory bulb of the brain.  
  “An olfactory receptor, which is a dendrite of a specialized neuron, responds when it binds certain molecules inhaled from the environment by sending impulses directly to the olfactory bulb of the brain.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.3 Taste and Smell, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-3-taste-and-smell)
- **Structure and job:** Olfactory neurons have a limited lifespan of about one to four months, and new ones are made regularly to take their place.  
  “The sensory neurons of the olfactory epithelium have a limited lifespan of approximately one to four months, and new ones are made on a regular basis.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 13.4 The Peripheral Nervous System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/13-4-the-peripheral-nervous-system)
- **Structure and job:** Because airborne toxic chemicals can harm the olfactory cells, the neurons are regularly replaced, and each new axon must find its connection in the olfactory bulb.  
  “The nasal epithelium, including the olfactory cells, can be harmed by airborne toxic chemicals. Therefore, the olfactory neurons are regularly replaced within the nasal epithelium, after which the axons of the new neurons must find their appropriate connections in the olfactory bulb.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
