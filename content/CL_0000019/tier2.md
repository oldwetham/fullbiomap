A sperm, or spermatozoon, is the male gamete: a reproductive cell that contributes genetic material to form offspring. At fertilization, a sperm and an oocyte, or egg, combine and their nuclei fuse; the sperm is haploid, carrying half of the genetic material needed to form a human being. Sperm form inside the testes, in tubes called seminiferous tubules; this process is called spermatogenesis. In the epididymis, a coiled tube where sperm are stored until ejaculation, sperm mature further and gain the ability to move on their own. A sperm is divided into a head, a mid-piece and a tail. The head holds a tightly packed nucleus and very little cytoplasm, the material between the cell membrane and the nucleus; these features help keep the sperm small. A cap called the acrosome covers most of the head and is filled with enzymes, proteins that speed up chemical reactions. The mid-piece is filled with tightly packed mitochondria, the cell parts that produce ATP, the cell's main energy-carrying molecule. ATP from these mitochondria powers the flagellum, a long, hair-like structure that runs from the mid-piece through the tail and moves the whole sperm. In the acrosomal reaction, the acrosome releases its stored enzymes, which clear a path for the sperm through the zona pellucida, the thick, gel-like layer that coats the egg.
