Each palatine tonsil traps and destroys pathogens (agents that can cause disease) entering the body through the mouth or nose. The main job of tonsils seems to be helping children's bodies recognize, destroy and build immunity to common pathogens in the environment, so that children are protected later in life. The palatine tonsils are a pair, one on each side of the oropharynx, the part of the throat behind the mouth. Each palatine tonsil sits between two arches: the palatoglossal arch in front, next to the base of the tongue, and the palatopharyngeal arch farther back. Lymphocytes, a type of white blood cell, are among the cells in the tonsils that protect against harmful substances and pathogens. Tonsils are lymphoid nodules, which are dense clusters of lymphocytes. The surface layer of each tonsil folds deep into the tonsil's interior, forming tonsillar crypts. These crypts collect all sorts of material taken in through eating and breathing, and they encourage pathogens to move deep into the tonsil, where the pathogens are eliminated.
