# Kidney collecting duct principal cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Special structure:** aquaporin water channels in the apical membrane (inserted when ADH stimulates the cell)  
  “When stimulated by ADH, aquaporin channels are inserted into the apical membrane of principal cells, which line the collecting ducts.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Special structure:** Na+/K+ ATPase pumps on the basal membrane  
  “Aldosterone stimulates principal cells to manufacture luminal Na+ and K+ channels as well as Na+/K+ ATPase pumps on the basal membrane of the cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Special structure:** luminal Na+ and K+ channels  
  “Aldosterone stimulates principal cells to manufacture luminal Na+ and K+ channels as well as Na+/K+ ATPase pumps on the basal membrane of the cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Special structure:** ADH receptors  
  “In addition to receptors for ADH, principal cells have receptors for the steroid hormone aldosterone.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Special structure:** aldosterone receptors  
  “In addition to receptors for ADH, principal cells have receptors for the steroid hormone aldosterone.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Structure and job:** Aquaporin proteins moved into its apical membrane form water channels, letting water pass through the cell from the collecting-duct lumen into the interstitial space of the medulla by osmosis.  
  “stimulating the movement of aquaporin proteins into the apical cell membrane of principal cells of the collecting ducts to form water channels, allowing the transcellular movement of water from the lumen of the collecting duct into the interstitial space in the medulla of the kidney by osmosis.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.8 Endocrine Regulation of Kidney Function, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-8-endocrine-regulation-of-kidney-function)
- **Structure and job:** Its channels for sodium and potassium let the principal cell either recover or lose these two ions.  
  “A principal cell possesses channels for the recovery or loss of sodium and potassium.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Structure and job:** Aldosterone has the cell build luminal Na+ and K+ channels and basal Na+/K+ ATPase pumps, so more Na+ is recovered from the forming urine and water follows it passively.  
  “Aldosterone stimulates principal cells to manufacture luminal Na+ and K+ channels as well as Na+/K+ ATPase pumps on the basal membrane of the cells. When aldosterone output increases, more Na+ is recovered from the forming urine and water follows the Na+ passively.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Structure and job:** Further channels in the cell secrete K+ into the collecting duct in direct proportion to how much Na+ it recovers.  
  “Still other channels in the principal cells secrete K+ into the collecting duct in direct proportion to the recovery of Na+.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
