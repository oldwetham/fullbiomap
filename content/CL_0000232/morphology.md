# Erythrocyte: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** biconcave disc  
  “Erythrocytes are biconcave disks; that is, they are plump at their periphery and very thin in the center” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Shape:** biconcave disc  
  “In mammals, mature erythrocytes are biconcave disks containing hemoglobin whose function is to transport oxygen.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000232 erythrocyte, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000232)
- **Size:** 7–8 µm diameter  
  “they are quite small cells, with a mean diameter of only about 7–8 micrometers (µm)” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Nuclei:** 0  
  “although mature erythrocytes do not have a nucleus” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.1 An Overview of Blood, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-1-an-overview-of-blood)
- **Nuclei:** 0  
  “As an erythrocyte matures in the red bone marrow, it extrudes its nucleus and most of its other organelles.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Organelles:** mitochondria: 0 absent  
  “Because erythrocytes do not contain mitochondria, glycolysis is the sole method by which these cells produce ATP.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 22.5 Transport of Gases, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-5-transport-of-gases)
- **Organelles:** rough ER: 0 absent  
  “They also lack endoplasmic reticula and do not synthesize proteins.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Organelles:** smooth ER: 0 absent  
  “They also lack endoplasmic reticula and do not synthesize proteins.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Special structure:** hemoglobin  
  “erythrocytes are red, biconcave disks packed with an oxygen-carrying compound called hemoglobin” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Special structure:** spectrin (cytoskeletal protein)  
  “Erythrocytes do, however, contain some structural proteins that help the blood cells maintain their unique structure and enable them to change their shape to squeeze through capillaries. This includes the protein spectrin, a cytoskeletal protein element.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Special structure:** surface glycoproteins and glycolipids (blood-group antigens)  
  “In humans, the surface glycoproteins and glycolipids on red blood cells vary between individuals, producing the different blood types, such as A, B, and O.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.2 Components of the Blood, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood)
- **Special structure:** rouleaux (in wider vessels, cells may stack like a roll of coins)  
  “In wider vessels, erythrocytes may stack up much like a roll of coins, forming a rouleaux” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Structure and job:** The biconcave shape gives the cell more membrane area for gas exchange relative to its volume than a sphere of the same diameter would have.  
  “The biconcave shape also provides a greater surface area across which gas exchange can occur, relative to its volume; a sphere of a similar diameter would have a lower surface area-to-volume ratio.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Structure and job:** Lacking most organelles, the cell has more interior space for the hemoglobin molecules that transport gases.  
  “Since they lack most organelles, there is more interior space for the presence of the hemoglobin molecules that, as you will see shortly, transport gases.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Structure and job:** With no mitochondria the cell relies on anaerobic respiration, so it uses none of the oxygen it carries and can deliver all of it to the tissues.  
  “Lacking mitochondria, for example, they rely on anaerobic respiration. This means that they do not utilize any of the oxygen they are transporting, so they can deliver it all to the tissues.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Structure and job:** Flexible structural proteins such as spectrin let the cell bend over on itself to a surprising degree and spring back when it enters a wider vessel.  
  “Fortunately, their structural proteins like spectrin are flexible, allowing them to bend over themselves to a surprising degree, then spring back again when they enter a wider vessel.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Structure and job:** Holding about 300 million hemoglobin molecules lets a single erythrocyte carry up to 1.2 billion oxygen molecules.  
  “An individual erythrocyte may contain about 300 million hemoglobin molecules, and therefore can bind to and transport up to 1.2 billion oxygen molecules” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Structure and job:** The iron in its hemoglobin picks up oxygen in the lungs and releases it in the tissues, and the hemoglobin's amino acids carry carbon dioxide back to the lungs.  
  “In the bloodstream, iron picks up oxygen in the lungs and drops it off in the tissues; the amino acids in hemoglobin then transport carbon dioxide from the tissues back to the lungs.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.3 Erythrocytes, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes)
- **Structure and job:** Its small size and large surface area let oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse quickly across its plasma membrane.  
  “The small size and large surface area of red blood cells allows for rapid diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide across the plasma membrane.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.2 Components of the Blood, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood)
- **Structure and job:** Bicarbonate is made mostly inside erythrocytes, where carbonic anhydrase joins carbon dioxide and water into carbonic acid, which breaks down into bicarbonate and hydrogen ions.  
  “Bicarbonate is created by a chemical reaction that occurs mostly in erythrocytes, joining carbon dioxide and water by carbonic anhydrase, producing carbonic acid, which breaks down into bicarbonate and hydrogen ions.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 22.5 Transport of Gases, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-5-transport-of-gases)
