The axon carries signals away from a neuron, or nerve cell, to other neurons, muscles or glands. A neuron usually has just one axon, which comes out of the cell body, the main part of the neuron. Many axons are wrapped in myelin, a fatty coating that helps signals travel faster. The axon ends in axon terminals, which release chemicals that pass the signal on to other cells.
