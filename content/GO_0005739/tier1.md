Mitochondria make ATP, the cell's main energy-carrying molecule. They do this by cellular respiration, which uses oxygen and the energy stored in nutrients. Their inner membrane has many folds, called cristae, which add surface area for making ATP. Muscles use huge amounts of ATP, so muscle cells are packed full of mitochondria.
