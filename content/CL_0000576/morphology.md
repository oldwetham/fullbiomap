# Monocyte: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Size:** 12–20 µm diameter  
  “Monocytes originate from myeloid stem cells. They normally represent 2–8 percent of the total leukocyte count. They are typically easily recognized by their large size of 12–20 µm and indented or horseshoe-shaped nuclei.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Nuclei:** 1  
  “Myeloid mononuclear recirculating leukocyte that can act as a precursor of tissue macrophages, osteoclasts and some populations of tissue dendritic cells.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000576 monocyte, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000576)
- **Nucleus shape:** horseshoe  
  “Monocytes originate from myeloid stem cells. They normally represent 2–8 percent of the total leukocyte count. They are typically easily recognized by their large size of 12–20 µm and indented or horseshoe-shaped nuclei.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** A recirculating white blood cell with a single nucleus, the monocyte acts as a precursor that can become a tissue macrophage, an osteoclast or some kinds of tissue dendritic cell.  
  “Myeloid mononuclear recirculating leukocyte that can act as a precursor of tissue macrophages, osteoclasts and some populations of tissue dendritic cells.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000576 monocyte, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000576)
- **Structure and job:** Once it leaves the bloodstream, a monocyte becomes a macrophage that engulfs debris, foreign pathogens, worn-out red blood cells and other dead or damaged cells.  
  “Macrophages are monocytes that have left the circulation and phagocytize debris, foreign pathogens, worn-out erythrocytes, and many other dead, worn out, or damaged cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** Monocytes travel in the blood and lymph and turn into macrophages after they move into infected tissue.  
  “A monocyte is a type of white blood cell that circulates in the blood and lymph and differentiates into macrophages after it moves into infected tissue.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.1 Innate Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-1-innate-immune-response)
