A podocyte wraps finger-like branches, called pedicels, around capillaries, the smallest blood vessels, to help form a filter. Podocytes are kidney cells found inside a glomerulus, a tuft of capillaries that filters the blood based on size. Bowman's capsule, the structure that encloses the glomerulus, has an outer layer that continues onto the glomerular capillaries and wraps closely around them as an inner layer. Podocytes are the uniquely shaped cells that make up this inner layer, called the visceral layer. The pedicels interlock, leaving small gaps called filtration slits that act as a sieve. In places, fenestrations in the capillaries, small windows that allow rapid filtration based on size, line up with these slits. There, only a shared basement membrane, a thin layer of fibrous material, separates the inside of the capillary from the inside of Bowman's capsule. Together, the fenestrations, the slits and the basement membrane make up the filtration membrane. As blood passes through the glomerulus, some plasma, the liquid part of blood, filters between the pedicels into Bowman's capsule. However, blood cells and medium-to-large proteins cannot pass through the fenestrations or between the pedicels.
