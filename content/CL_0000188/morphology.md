- **Special structure:** myofibrils: long cylinders about 1.2 µm in diameter, parallel to the fiber and running its entire length  
  “Within each muscle fiber are myofibrils—long cylindrical structures that lie parallel to the muscle fiber.   “Each sarcomere is approximately 2 μm in length with a three-dimensional cylinder-like arrangement and is bordered by structures called Z-discs (also called Z-lines, because pictures are two-dimensional), to which the actin myofilaments are anchored” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.2 Skeletal Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)

  “Dark A bands and light I bands repeat along myofibrils, and the alignment of myofibrils in the cell causes the entire cell to appear striated or banded.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 38.4 Muscle Contraction and Locomotion, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/38-4-muscle-contraction-and-locomotion)

- **Special structure:** T tubules: periodic invaginations of the sarcolemma  
  “For the action potential to reach the membrane of the SR, there are periodic invaginations in the sarcolemma, called T-tubules (“T” stands for “transverse”).” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.2 Skeletal Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)

  “The T tubules are only found at the Z discs, whereas in skeletal muscle, they are found at the junction of the A and I bands.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 19.2 Cardiac Muscle and Electrical Activity, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-2-cardiac-muscle-and-electrical-activity)

  “Each I band has a dense line running vertically through the middle called a Z disc or Z line.   “This central region of the A band looks slightly lighter than the rest of the A band and is called the H zone.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 38.4 Muscle Contraction and Locomotion, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/38-4-muscle-contraction-and-locomotion)
- **Special structure:** M line: line at the middle of the H zone where accessory proteins hold the thick filaments together  
  “The middle of the H zone has a vertical line called the M line, at which accessory proteins hold together thick filaments.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 38.4 Muscle Contraction and Locomotion, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/38-4-muscle-contraction-and-locomotion)
- **Special structure:** triads: a T tubule with sarcoplasmic reticulum membranes on either side, surrounding a myofibril  
  “The arrangement of a T-tubule with the membranes of SR on either side is called a triad (). The triad surrounds the cylindrical structure called a myofibril, which contains actin and myosin.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.2 Skeletal Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)
- **Special structure:** sarcoplasmic reticulum: specialized smooth ER that stores, releases and retrieves calcium ions  
  “the specialized smooth endoplasmic reticulum, which stores, releases, and retrieves calcium ions (Ca++) is called the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR)” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.2 Skeletal Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)
- **Special structure:** neuromuscular junction: every fiber is innervated by a motor neuron  
  “Every skeletal muscle fiber in every skeletal muscle is innervated by a motor neuron at the NMJ.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.2 Skeletal Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)
- **Special structure:** motor end plate with junctional folds of the sarcolemma  
  “The motor end plate possesses junctional folds—folds in the sarcolemma that create a large surface area for the neurotransmitter to bind to receptors.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 38.4 Muscle Contraction and Locomotion, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/38-4-muscle-contraction-and-locomotion)

  “Inside each fascicle, each muscle fiber is encased in a thin connective tissue layer of collagen and reticular fibers called the endomysium.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.2 Skeletal Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)
- **Structure and job:** Having many nuclei gives the fiber many copies of its genes, so it can make the large amounts of proteins and enzymes that contraction needs.  
  “Multiple nuclei mean multiple copies of genes, permitting the production of the large amounts of proteins and enzymes needed for muscle contraction.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.2 Skeletal Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)
- **Structure and job:** Because its myofibrils attach to the cell membrane at their ends, their shortening shortens the whole fiber.  
  “They attach to the sarcolemma at their ends, so that as myofibrils shorten, the entire muscle cell contracts” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 38.4 Muscle Contraction and Locomotion, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/38-4-muscle-contraction-and-locomotion)
- **Structure and job:** T tubules carry the action potential into the interior of the fiber, where it opens calcium channels in the adjacent sarcoplasmic reticulum and releases the calcium that starts contraction.  
  “The T-tubules carry the action potential into the interior of the cell, which triggers the opening of calcium channels in the membrane of the adjacent SR, causing Ca++ to diffuse out of the SR and into the sarcoplasm. It is the arrival of Ca++ in the sarcoplasm that initiates contraction” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.2 Skeletal Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)
- **Structure and job:** Because the fiber can be up to 100 µm wide, T tubules bring the surface membrane close to the sarcoplasmic reticulum inside the cell.  
  “You will recall that the diameter of a muscle fiber can be up to 100 μm, so these T-tubules ensure that the membrane can get close to the SR in the sarcoplasm.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.2 Skeletal Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)
