Alpha granules are tiny sacs, each wrapped in a membrane, that hold proteins and release them. These sacs sit inside platelets, small bits of cells that form a plug at wounds to stop bleeding. Some alpha granule proteins are made mainly by megakaryocytes, the bone marrow cells that make platelets. Other alpha granule proteins are taken in by endocytosis, which moves substances into a cell.
