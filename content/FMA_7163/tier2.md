The skin protects the body, senses changes in the environment, helps control body temperature and makes vitamin D. The skin surrounds the entire body and forms the body's outer layer. The skin's outermost layer, the epidermis, is fairly thin and has no blood supply. The main type of cell in the epidermis produces keratin, a protein that makes skin hard and water-resistant. The top layer of the epidermis is made of dead cells, which are shed from time to time and replaced by cells made in the deepest layer. Cells called melanocytes make melanin, a pigment that gives skin its color and helps protect living cells in the epidermis from ultraviolet (UV) radiation. Below the epidermis lies the much thicker dermis, which contains blood vessels, sweat glands and hair follicles, the sacs from which hairs grow. Fibers of collagen and elastin in the dermis give the skin strength and elasticity. Nerve structures in the layers of the skin detect touch, surface temperature and pain, so the skin works as a sense organ. When the body warms up, blood vessels in the dermis widen and sweat glands release more sweat, and evaporation of that sweat cools the body.
