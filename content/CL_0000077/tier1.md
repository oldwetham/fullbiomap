Mesothelial cells make and release lubricants that cut friction between nearby tissues during movement. These flat cells form the mesothelium, the tissue that covers the major body cavities. In the pleura, the membrane that covers the lungs, these cells release a fluid that cuts friction as we breathe. Around the heart, the mesothelium releases a fluid that reduces friction as the heart contracts.
