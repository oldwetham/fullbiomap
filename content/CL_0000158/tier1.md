Club cells protect the lining of the tracheobronchial tree, which is made up of the windpipe and the air tubes of the lungs. A club cell has no cilia, the small hair-like projections that line the walls of these air tubes. However, club cells can multiply and turn into cells that do have cilia. In this way, club cells rebuild the lining of the bronchioles, the small airways that spread through the lungs.
