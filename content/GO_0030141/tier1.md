Secretory granules are small sacs that hold concentrated protein for the cell to release, or secrete. They form from the Golgi apparatus, the cell part that changes, packs and ships proteins. Secretory granules move toward the outer edge of the cell. When the cell is stimulated, they fuse with its outer membrane and release their protein outside the cell.
