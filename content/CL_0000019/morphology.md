# Sperm: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Nuclei:** 1  
  “The head of the sperm contains the extremely compact haploid nucleus with very little cytoplasm.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Surface:** flagellum  
  “A flagellum (plural = flagella) is an appendage larger than a cilium and specialized for cell locomotion. The only flagellated cell in humans is the sperm cell that must propel itself towards female egg cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 3.2 The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/3-2-the-cytoplasm-and-cellular-organelles)
- **Organelles:** mitochondria: 3 abundant  
  “Tightly packed mitochondria fill the mid-piece of the sperm.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Special structure:** acrosome  
  “A structure called the acrosome covers most of the head of the sperm cell as a “cap” that is filled with lysosomal enzymes important for preparing sperm to participate in fertilization.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Special structure:** axial filament  
  “The central strand of the flagellum, the axial filament, is formed from one centriole inside the maturing sperm cell during the final stages of spermatogenesis.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** Mitochondria packed into the midpiece supply the ATP that powers the flagellum, which runs from the neck through the tail and moves the whole cell.  
  “Tightly packed mitochondria fill the mid-piece of the sperm. ATP produced by these mitochondria will power the flagellum, which extends from the neck and the mid-piece through the tail of the sperm, enabling it to move the entire sperm cell.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 27.1 Anatomy and Physiology of the Testicular Reproductive System, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-testicular-reproductive-system)
- **Structure and job:** The acrosome, the sperm's enzyme-filled cap, releases its stored digestive enzymes to clear a path through the zona pellucida so the sperm can reach the oocyte.  
  “This initiates a process called the acrosomal reaction in which the enzyme-filled “cap” of the sperm, called the acrosome, releases its stored digestive enzymes. These enzymes clear a path through the zona pellucida that allows sperm to reach the oocyte.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 28.1 Fertilization, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/28-1-fertilization)
- **Structure and job:** The sperm carries a haploid nucleus, which it releases into the oocyte once it breaks through to the oocyte's plasma membrane.  
  “One of the sperm will then break through to the oocyte’s plasma membrane and release its haploid nucleus into the oocyte.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 28.1 Fertilization, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/28-1-fertilization)
