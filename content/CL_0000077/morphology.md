# Mesothelial cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Nuclei:** 1  
  “Squamous epithelial cells are generally round, flat, and have a small, centrally located nucleus.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 33.2 Animal Primary Tissues, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/33-2-animal-primary-tissues)
- **Nucleus shape:** flattened  
  “Squamous cell nuclei tend to be flat, horizontal, and elliptical, mirroring the form of the cell.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.2 Epithelial Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-2-epithelial-tissue)
- **Nucleus position:** central  
  “Squamous epithelial cells are generally round, flat, and have a small, centrally located nucleus.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 33.2 Animal Primary Tissues, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/33-2-animal-primary-tissues)
- **Nucleus size:** small  
  “Squamous epithelial cells are generally round, flat, and have a small, centrally located nucleus.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 33.2 Animal Primary Tissues, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/33-2-animal-primary-tissues)
- **Structure and job:** By secreting serous fluid, the cells of the thin, squamous mesothelium lubricate the serous membrane and reduce abrasion and friction between organs.  
  “Serous fluid secreted by the cells of the thin squamous mesothelium lubricates the membrane and reduces abrasion and friction between organs.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.1 Types of Tissues, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-1-types-of-tissues)
- **Structure and job:** As a single layer of flat cells forming the surface of the serous membrane that lines body cavities and internal organs, the mesothelium mainly provides a smooth, protective surface.  
  “The mesothelium is a simple squamous epithelium that forms the surface layer of the serous membrane that lines body cavities and internal organs. Its primary function is to provide a smooth and protective surface.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.2 Epithelial Tissue, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-2-epithelial-tissue)
- **Structure and job:** Mesothelial cells synthesize and secrete lubricants, such as glycosaminoglycans and surfactants, that minimize friction between adjacent tissues as they move.  
  “This cell plays a crucial role in synthesizing and secreting lubricants, such as glycosaminoglycans and surfactants, which minimize friction between adjacent tissues during movement.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000077 mesothelial cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000077)
- **Structure and job:** The mesothelial cells of the pleural membrane make pleural fluid, which lubricates to reduce friction during breathing and acts as an adhesive holding the lungs to the thoracic wall, helping the lungs move during ventilation.  
  “The mesothelial cells of the pleural membrane create pleural fluid, which serves as both a lubricant (to reduce friction during breathing) and as an adhesive to adhere the lungs to the thoracic wall (to facilitate movement of the lungs during ventilation).” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 22.2 The Lungs, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-2-the-lungs)
- **Structure and job:** Mesothelial cells closely monitor the level of pleural fluid, which the lymphatic system drains, because too much or too little fluid would hinder the creation of the negative intrapleural pressure.  
  “Too much or too little pleural fluid would hinder the creation of the negative intrapleural pressure; therefore, the level must be closely monitored by the mesothelial cells and drained by the lymphatic system.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 22.3 The Process of Breathing, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-3-the-process-of-breathing)
- **Structure and job:** A simple squamous mesothelium secretes the lubricating serous fluid that fills the pericardial cavity, reducing friction as the heart contracts.  
  “consisting of a simple squamous epithelium called a mesothelium, reinforced with loose, irregular, or areolar connective tissue that attaches to the pericardium. This mesothelium secretes the lubricating serous fluid that fills the pericardial cavity and reduces friction as the heart contracts.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 19.1 Heart Anatomy, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-1-heart-anatomy)
