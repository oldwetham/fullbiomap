# Mast cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Granules:** histamine and heparin granules; look: unknown; abundance: many; position: unknown  
  “The mast cell, found in connective tissue proper, has many cytoplasmic granules. These granules contain the chemical signals histamine and heparin.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.3 Connective Tissue Supports and Protects, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-3-connective-tissue-supports-and-protects)
- **Granules:** basophilic granules; look: basophilic; abundance: many; position: unknown  
  “A cell that is found in almost all tissues containing numerous basophilic granules and capable of releasing large amounts of histamine and heparin upon activation.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000097 mast cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000097)
- **Special structure:** IgE-specific Fc receptors on the cell surface  
  “its Fc region binds strongly to an IgE-specific Fc receptor on the surfaces of mast cells” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.4 The Adaptive Immune Response: B-lymphocytes and Antibodies, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-4-the-adaptive-immune-response-b-lymphocytes-and-antibodies)
- **Structure and job:** Mast cells keep histamine and heparin in their granules and, when irritated or damaged, release the histamine, which dilates blood vessels and increases blood flow at a site of injury or infection.  
  “These granules contain the chemical signals histamine and heparin. When irritated or damaged, mast cells release histamine, an inflammatory mediator, which causes vasodilation and increased blood flow at a site of injury or infection” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.3 Connective Tissue Supports and Protects, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-3-connective-tissue-supports-and-protects)
- **Structure and job:** When the IgE held on a mast cell binds its antigen, the cell releases histamine and serotonin, and these mediators recruit eosinophils that carry out the allergic response.  
  “IgE molecules on mast cells bind the antigen via their variable domains and stimulate the mast cell to release the modified amino acids histamine and serotonin; these chemical mediators then recruit eosinophils which mediate allergic responses.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.4 Disruptions in the Immune System, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-4-disruptions-in-the-immune-system)
