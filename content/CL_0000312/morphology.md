# Keratinocyte: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Nuclei:** 0 (once dead: the nucleus disintegrates as the cell dies)  
  “The nuclei and other cell organelles disintegrate as the cells die, leaving behind the keratin, keratohyalin, and cell membranes that will form the stratum lucidum, the stratum corneum, and the accessory structures of hair and nails.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Special structure:** keratin  
  “A keratinocyte is a cell that manufactures and stores the protein keratin.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Special structure:** desmosomes (interlocking junctions that bind neighboring cells)  
  “As the name suggests, the stratum spinosum is spiny in appearance due to the protruding cell processes that join the cells via a structure called a desmosome. The desmosomes interlock with each other and strengthen the bond between the cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Special structure:** lamellar bodies (contain glycolipids that are secreted to the cell surface to form a waterproof barrier)  
  “they are different from the lamellar bodies, which contain glycolipids that are secreted to the cell surface and help to form a waterproof barrier to prevent fluid loss from the body.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Special structure:** thickened cell membrane (as the cell flattens in the stratum granulosum)  
  “The cells (three to five layers deep) become flatter, their cell membranes thicken” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Special structure:** eleidin (clear protein derived from keratohyalin that densely packs the dead keratinocytes of the stratum lucidum, making them translucent)  
  “The keratinocytes that compose the stratum lucidum are dead and flattened (see ). These cells are densely packed with eleidin, a clear protein, derived from keratohyalin, which gives these cells their transparent (i.e., lucid) appearance.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Special structure:** melanin (made by melanocytes and transferred into the keratinocyte in melanosomes)  
  “Recall that melanin is produced by cells called melanocytes, which are found scattered throughout the stratum basale of the epidermis. The melanin is transferred into the keratinocytes via a cellular vesicle called a melanosome” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** It makes and stores keratin, a fibrous protein that gives skin, hair and nails their hardness and water resistance.  
  “A keratinocyte is a cell that manufactures and stores the protein keratin. Keratin is an intracellular fibrous protein that gives hair, nails, and skin their hardness and water-resistant properties.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** In the stratum spinosum it releases a water-repelling glycolipid that limits water loss, making the skin relatively waterproof.  
  “The keratinocytes in the stratum spinosum begin the synthesis of keratin and release a water-repelling glycolipid that helps prevent water loss from the body, making the skin relatively waterproof.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** By producing keratin and secreting antimicrobial peptides, it builds a tough barrier against damage, dehydration, pathogens and microbes.  
  “that produces keratin proteins and secretes antimicrobial peptides to form a resilient barrier against environmental damage, dehydration, pathogens, and microbial invasion.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000312 keratinocyte, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000312)
- **Structure and job:** Melanin that builds up inside keratinocytes darkens the skin and protects the DNA of epidermal cells from UV damage.  
  “The accumulation of melanin in keratinocytes results in the darkening of the skin, or a tan. This increased melanin accumulation protects the DNA of epidermal cells from UV ray damage and the breakdown of folic acid” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** Dead keratinocytes at the surface slough away and are replaced by cells from deeper layers, so the outer layer keeps renewing.  
  “The keratinocytes in the stratum corneum are dead and regularly slough away, being replaced by cells from the deeper layers” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.1 Layers of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** Densely packed dead keratinocytes make up the nails, which protect the fingers and toes from mechanical stress.  
  “Nails are composed of densely packed dead keratinocytes. They protect the fingers and toes from mechanical stress.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.2 Accessory Structures of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-2-accessory-structures-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** Keratinocytes made by basal cells of the hair bulb build the hair, pushing the shaft up through the follicle as new cells are added.  
  “Hair growth begins with the production of keratinocytes by the basal cells of the hair bulb. As new cells are deposited at the hair bulb, the hair shaft is pushed through the follicle toward the surface.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 5.2 Accessory Structures of the Skin, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-2-accessory-structures-of-the-skin)
- **Structure and job:** As it matures it passes through successive stages of differentiation with changing keratin expression, which supports tissue integrity and wound repair and contributes to immune defense.  
  “It undergoes successive stages of differentiation marked by changes in keratin expression, supporting tissue integrity, wound repair, and contributing to immune defense.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000312 keratinocyte, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000312)
