Intestinal villus goblet cells make mucus, a thick, slippery substance that keeps tissues moist. These cells release the mucus, along with fluid, into the hollow space inside the gut. These goblet cells sit in the surface layer of the villi, projections of the small intestine's lining. Each goblet cell is a one-cell gland tucked between column-shaped cells.
