A non-myelinating Schwann cell wraps around several thin axons, the parts of nerve cells that carry signals. As its name says, it does not coat these axons in myelin, a fatty insulating layer. The non-myelinating Schwann cell is a glial cell, a cell that supports nerve cells. It works in the peripheral nervous system: every part of the nervous system except the brain and spinal cord.
