Megakaryocytes break apart to form platelets, small cell fragments that collect at wounds and form a plug to prevent blood loss. A lung megakaryocyte is a megakaryocyte that lives in the lung's connective tissue, the type of tissue that holds the body's organs in place and connects them. A platelet is not a whole cell but a fragment of a megakaryocyte's cytoplasm, the material between the cell membrane and the nucleus, wrapped in membrane. Megakaryocytes are large cells, typically 50–100 µm across, with an enlarged, lobed nucleus. Megakaryocytes come from myeloid stem cells, which also give rise to red blood cells and several kinds of white blood cells. Thrombopoietin, a substance made by the kidneys and liver, makes cells called megakaryoblasts multiply, and the megakaryoblasts then mature into megakaryocytes.
