# Kidney collecting duct intercalated cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Special structure:** apical proton pumps  
  “In the collecting ducts, the apical surfaces of intercalated cells have proton pumps that actively secrete H+ into the luminal, forming urine to remove it from the body.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.9 Regulation of Fluid Volume and Composition, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-9-regulation-of-fluid-volume-and-composition)
- **Special structure:** array of membrane pumps and channels  
  “An intercalated cell secretes or absorbs acid or bicarbonate. As in other portions of the nephron, there is an array of micromachines (pumps and channels) on display in the membranes of these cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
- **Structure and job:** Proton pumps on its apical surface actively secrete H+ into the forming urine, so the acid leaves the body in the urine.  
  “In the collecting ducts, the apical surfaces of intercalated cells have proton pumps that actively secrete H+ into the luminal, forming urine to remove it from the body.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.9 Regulation of Fluid Volume and Composition, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-9-regulation-of-fluid-volume-and-composition)
- **Structure and job:** By reabsorbing K+ and bicarbonate (HCO3–) while secreting H+, it makes the plasma less acidic and the urine more acidic, which helps regulate blood pH.  
  “Intercalated cells play significant roles in regulating blood pH. Intercalated cells reabsorb K+ and HCO3– while secreting H+. This function lowers the acidity of the plasma while increasing the acidity of the urine.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 25.6 Tubular Reabsorption, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption)
