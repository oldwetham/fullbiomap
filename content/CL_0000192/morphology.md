# Smooth muscle cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** spindle  
  “Smooth muscle fibers are spindle-shaped (wide in the middle and tapered at both ends, somewhat like a football)” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Size:** 30–200 µm length  
  “Smooth muscle fibers are spindle-shaped (wide in the middle and tapered at both ends, somewhat like a football) and have a single nucleus; they range from about 30 to 200 μm (thousands of times shorter than skeletal muscle fibers)” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Nuclei:** 1  
  “Smooth muscle cells have a single nucleus, and are spindle-shaped.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Nucleus position:** central  
  “Smooth muscle does not have striations in its cells. It has a single, centrally located nucleus” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 33.2 Animal Primary Tissues, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/33-2-animal-primary-tissues)
- **Organelles:** myofibrils: 0 absent  
  “Smooth muscles differ from skeletal and cardiac muscles in that they ________. lack myofibrils are under voluntary control lack myosin lack actin A” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Special structure:** dense bodies  
  “These thin filaments are anchored by dense bodies. A dense body is analogous to the Z-discs of skeletal and cardiac muscle fibers and is fastened to the sarcolemma.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Special structure:** intermediate filament network  
  “When the thin filaments slide past the thick filaments, they pull on the dense bodies, structures tethered to the sarcolemma, which then pull on the intermediate filaments networks throughout the sarcoplasm.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Special structure:** thick and thin (myosin and actin) filaments, not arranged into sarcomeres or striations  
  “Although they do not have striations and sarcomeres, smooth muscle fibers do have actin and myosin contractile proteins, and thick and thin filaments.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Special structure:** caveolae (membrane indentations; the source spells them "calveoli")  
  “Calcium ions are supplied by the SR in the fibers and by sequestration from the extracellular fluid through membrane indentations called calveoli.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Special structure:** gap junctions (between fibers of single-unit smooth muscle)  
  “Single-unit muscle has its muscle fibers joined by gap junctions so that the muscle contracts as a single unit.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Special structure:** endomysium (connective tissue the cells produce themselves)  
  “they produce their own connective tissue, endomysium” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Structure and job:** Because its filaments pull on dense bodies tethered to the cell membrane, which in turn pull on an intermediate-filament network spanning the cytoplasm, the whole cell contracts with its ends drawn toward the center.  
  “they pull on the dense bodies, structures tethered to the sarcolemma, which then pull on the intermediate filaments networks throughout the sarcoplasm. This arrangement causes the entire muscle fiber to contract in a manner whereby the ends are pulled toward the center” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Structure and job:** The cell is narrow enough that it needs no T tubules to carry an action potential deep into its interior.  
  “smooth muscle fibers have a much smaller diameter than skeletal muscle cells. T-tubules are not required to reach the interior of the cell and therefore not necessary to transmit an action potential deep into the fiber.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Structure and job:** Because its actin and myosin filaments are not rigidly organized, the cell can contract over a wider range of resting lengths than skeletal or cardiac muscle cells.  
  “Smooth muscles can contract over a wider range of resting lengths because the actin and myosin filaments in smooth muscle are not as rigidly organized as those in skeletal and cardiac muscle.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Structure and job:** Because sarcomeres do not limit how far they can stretch, visceral smooth muscle cells give a stress-relaxation response as a hollow organ fills and stretches them.  
  “Because the muscle fibers are not constrained by the organization and stretchability limits of sarcomeres, visceral smooth muscle has a stress-relaxation response. This means that as the muscle of a hollow organ is stretched when it fills” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Structure and job:** Gap junctions between single-unit smooth muscle cells synchronize their depolarization, so the muscle they form contracts as a single unit.  
  “Single-unit smooth muscle tissue contains gap junctions to synchronize membrane depolarization and contractions so that the muscle contracts as a single unit.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 10.8 Smooth Muscle, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)
- **Structure and job:** Arranged in rings around the start of a capillary as precapillary sphincters, smooth muscle cells tightly control how much blood flows into the capillary bed.  
  “The precapillary sphincters, circular smooth muscle cells that surround the capillary at its origin with the metarteriole, tightly regulate the flow of blood from a metarteriole to the capillaries it supplies.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 20.1 Structure and Function of Blood Vessels, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-1-structure-and-function-of-blood-vessels)
- **Structure and job:** Spindle-shaped smooth muscle cells in the walls of organs and passageways contract involuntarily to move food and secretions and to regulate blood flow in arteries.  
  “Smooth | Short, spindle-shaped, no evident striation, single nucleus in each fiber | Involuntary movement, moves food, involuntary control of respiration, moves secretions, regulates flow of blood in arteries by contraction | Walls of major organs and passageways” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 4.4 Muscle Tissue and Motion, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-4-muscle-tissue-and-motion)
