# Platelet: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** disc  
  “Each platelet is disc shaped and 2–4 μm in diameter.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.2 Components of the Blood, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood)
- **Shape:** other: spiked, on meeting a vessel rupture  
  “platelets, which normally float free in the plasma, encounter the area of vessel rupture with the exposed underlying connective tissue and collagenous fibers. The platelets begin to clump together, become spiked and sticky, and bind to the exposed collagen and endothelial lining.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.5 Hemostasis, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-5-hemostasis)
- **Size:** 2–4 µm diameter  
  “Platelets are relatively small, 2–4 µm in diameter, but numerous, with typically 150,000–160,000 per µL of blood.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Size:** 2–4 µm diameter  
  “Each platelet is disc shaped and 2–4 μm in diameter.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.2 Components of the Blood, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood)
- **Nuclei:** 0  
  “They contain many small vesicles but do not contain a nucleus.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.2 Components of the Blood, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood)
- **Nuclei:** 0  
  “MHC I molecules are proteins on the surfaces of all nucleated cells, thus they are scarce on red blood cells and platelets which are non-nucleated.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.1 Innate Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-1-innate-immune-response)
- **Granules:** small vesicles; look: small; abundance: many; position: unknown  
  “They contain many small vesicles but do not contain a nucleus.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.2 Components of the Blood, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood)
- **Special structure:** cell fragment: a piece of megakaryocyte cytoplasm enclosed by plasma membrane, not a true cell  
  “A platelet is not a cell but rather a fragment of the cytoplasm of a cell called a megakaryocyte that is surrounded by a plasma membrane.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Special structure:** contractile proteins  
  “The stabilized clot is acted upon by contractile proteins within the platelets.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.5 Hemostasis, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-5-hemostasis)
- **Structure and job:** At a torn vessel, platelets become spiked and sticky, clump together and bind to the exposed collagen and endothelial lining.  
  “platelets, which normally float free in the plasma, encounter the area of vessel rupture with the exposed underlying connective tissue and collagenous fibers. The platelets begin to clump together, become spiked and sticky, and bind to the exposed collagen and endothelial lining.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.5 Hemostasis, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-5-hemostasis)
- **Structure and job:** Platelets gathered into a plug can temporarily seal a small opening in a blood vessel, buying time for more durable repairs.  
  “A platelet plug can temporarily seal a small opening in a blood vessel. Plug formation, in essence, buys the body time while more sophisticated and durable repairs are being made.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.5 Hemostasis, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-5-hemostasis)
- **Structure and job:** Platelets hold chemicals in granules and release them into the plasma as they gather at an injury, which furthers hemostasis.  
  “As platelets collect, they simultaneously release chemicals from their granules into the plasma that further contribute to hemostasis.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.5 Hemostasis, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-5-hemostasis)
- **Structure and job:** By extending many projections, platelets stick at a wound and release their contents, which activate other platelets and interact with coagulation factors.  
  “Small cell fragments called platelets (thrombocytes) are attracted to the wound site where they adhere by extending many projections and releasing their contents. These contents activate other platelets and also interact with other coagulation factors” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.2 Components of the Blood, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood)
- **Structure and job:** Contractile proteins inside platelets pull on the fibrin threads of a stabilized clot, drawing its edges more tightly together.  
  “The stabilized clot is acted upon by contractile proteins within the platelets. As these proteins contract, they pull on the fibrin threads, bringing the edges of the clot more tightly together” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.5 Hemostasis, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-5-hemostasis)
- **Structure and job:** Besides stopping blood loss, platelets secrete growth factors that support the growth and repair of tissue, especially connective tissue.  
  “Platelets are critical to hemostasis, the stoppage of blood loss following damage to a vessel. They also secrete a variety of growth factors essential for growth and repair of tissue, particularly connective tissue.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
