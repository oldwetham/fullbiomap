# Cochlear inner hair cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** other: bulbous  
  “A bulbous cell that is medially placed in one row in the organ of Corti.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000589 cochlear inner hair cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000589)
- **Surface:** stereocilia  
  “The stereocilia from inner hair cells extend into small dimples on the tectorial membrane’s lower surface.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Surface:** stereocilia  
  “The organs of Corti contain hair cells, which are named for the hair-like stereocilia extending from the cell’s apical surfaces” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** stereocilia array graded in height from tallest to shortest  
  “The stereocilia are an array of microvilli-like structures arranged from tallest to shortest.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** protein tethers linking adjacent stereocilia within each array  
  “Protein fibers tether adjacent hairs together within each array, such that the array will bend in response to movements of the basilar membrane.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Special structure:** fewer sensory hairs than outer hair cells  
  “In contrast to the outer hair cells, the inner hair cells are fewer in number, have fewer sensory hairs, and are less differentiated.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000589 cochlear inner hair cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000589)
- **Special structure:** afferent synapses: each inner hair cell synapses with about 10 afferent neurons  
  “About 90 percent of the afferent neurons carry information from inner hair cells, with each hair cell synapsing with 10 or so neurons.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** Set in a single row of about 3,500 cells, the inner hair cells are the primary auditory receptors.  
  “The inner hair cells are the primary auditory receptors and exist in a single row, numbering approximately 3,500.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** The stereocilia reach up to the overlying tectorial membrane, so when pressure waves from the scala move the basilar membrane, the tectorial membrane slides across them.  
  “The stereocilia extend up from the hair cells to the overlying tectorial membrane, which is attached medially to the organ of Corti. When the pressure waves from the scala move the basilar membrane, the tectorial membrane slides across the stereocilia.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** The stereocilia are mechanoreceptors: when vibrations bend them, gated ion channels open, depolarizing the hair cell so a signal passes to the cochlear nerve.  
  “All of the stereocilia are mechanoreceptors, and when bent by vibrations they respond by opening a gated ion channel (refer to ). As a result, the hair cell membrane is depolarized, and a signal is transmitted to the chochlear nerve.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** Bending the stereocilia toward the tallest one puts tension on the protein tethers between them, opening ion channels that depolarize the cell and trigger impulses in the afferent nerve fibers attached to it.  
  “When the stereocilia bend toward the tallest member of their array, tension in the protein tethers opens ion channels in the hair cell membrane. This will depolarize the hair cell membrane, triggering nerve impulses that travel down the afferent nerve fibers attached to the hair cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Bending the stereocilia the other way, toward the shortest one, slackens the tethers and closes the ion channels.  
  “When the stereocilia bend toward the shortest member of their array, the tension on the tethers slackens and the ion channels close.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Even in silence, slight tension on the tethers between the upright stereocilia keeps the hair cell slightly depolarized.  
  “When no sound is present, and the stereocilia are standing straight, a small amount of tension still exists on the tethers, keeping the membrane potential of the hair cell slightly depolarized.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** When its hairs bend, the hair cell releases an excitatory neurotransmitter at a synapse with a sensory neuron, which then carries action potentials to the central nervous system.  
  “When the hairs bend, they release an excitatory neurotransmitter at a synapse with a sensory neuron, which then conducts action potentials to the central nervous system.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** Inner hair cells are the most important route for auditory information to the brain: about 90 percent of the afferent neurons carry their signals, and each cell synapses with about 10 of them.  
  “The inner hair cells are most important for conveying auditory information to the brain. About 90 percent of the afferent neurons carry information from inner hair cells, with each hair cell synapsing with 10 or so neurons.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** Because the tectorial membrane moves only where the basilar membrane moves, and each region of that membrane moves only at a specific frequency, the hair cells in a region respond only to that frequency.  
  “As stated above, a given region of the basilar membrane will only move if the incoming sound is at a specific frequency. Because the tectorial membrane only moves where the basilar membrane moves, the hair cells in this region will also only respond to sounds of this specific frequency.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
- **Structure and job:** Adjacent inner hair cells differ in response frequency by only about 0.2 percent, so neighboring cells respond to slightly different pitches.  
  “The difference in response frequency between adjacent inner hair cells is about 0.2 percent.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 36.4 Hearing and Vestibular Sensation, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/36-4-hearing-and-vestibular-sensation)
- **Structure and job:** A hair cell's place along the cochlea sets its pitch: cells at the apex detect frequencies as low as 20 Hz, and cells at the base, near the round and oval windows, encode frequencies around 20 kHz.  
  “Frequencies as low as 20 Hz are detected by hair cells at the apex, or tip, of the cochlea. Frequencies in the higher ranges of 20 KHz are encoded by hair cells at the base of the cochlea, close to the round and oval windows” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 14.1 Sensory Perception, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/14-1-sensory-perception)
