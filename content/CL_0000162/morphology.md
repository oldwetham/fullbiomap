# Parietal cell: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Shape:** pyramid  
  “This cell is characterized by its pyramidal shape” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000162 parietal cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000162)
- **Organelles:** mitochondria: 3 abundant  
  “This cell is characterized by its pyramidal shape, abundant mitochondria” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000162 parietal cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000162)
- **Special structure:** secretory canaliculi  
  “a complex network of secretory canaliculi lined with microvilli” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000162 parietal cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000162)
- **Structure and job:** A pyramidal cell with abundant mitochondria and a network of microvilli-lined secretory canaliculi, it secretes hydrochloric acid into the stomach lumen and produces intrinsic factor, which is essential for vitamin B12 absorption.  
  “This cell is characterized by its pyramidal shape, abundant mitochondria, and a complex network of secretory canaliculi lined with microvilli. It secretes hydrochloric acid into the stomach lumen and produces intrinsic factor, essential for vitamin B12 absorption.” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000162 parietal cell, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000162)
- **Structure and job:** Relatively large and among the body's most highly differentiated epithelial cells, parietal cells sit mainly in the middle region of the gastric glands and produce both hydrochloric acid and intrinsic factor.  
  “Located primarily in the middle region of the gastric glands are parietal cells, which are among the most highly differentiated of the body’s epithelial cells. These relatively large cells produce both hydrochloric acid (HCl) and intrinsic factor.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.4 The Stomach, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-4-the-stomach)
- **Structure and job:** The hydrochloric acid these cells produce gives the stomach contents their high acidity (pH 1.5 to 3.5) and is needed to activate the protein-digesting enzyme pepsin.  
  “These relatively large cells produce both hydrochloric acid (HCl) and intrinsic factor. HCl is responsible for the high acidity (pH 1.5 to 3.5) of the stomach contents and is needed to activate the protein-digesting enzyme, pepsin.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.4 The Stomach, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-4-the-stomach)
- **Structure and job:** Parietal cells secrete hydrogen and chloride ions, which combine in the lumen to form hydrochloric acid, the primary acidic component of stomach juices.  
  “Another cell type—parietal cells—secrete hydrogen and chloride ions, which combine in the lumen to form hydrochloric acid, the primary acidic component of the stomach juices.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 34.1 Digestive Systems, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/34-1-digestive-systems)
- **Structure and job:** Gastrin stimulates parietal cells in the stomach mucosa to secrete gastric acid.  
  “Gastrin stimulates the secretion of gastric acid by the parietal cells of the stomach mucosa.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.2 Digestive System Processes and Regulation, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-2-digestive-system-processes-and-regulation)
- **Structure and job:** Histamine, made by the stomach mucosa when food is in the stomach, stimulates parietal cells to release HCl.  
  “Histamine | Stomach mucosa | Presence of food in the stomach | Stomach | Stimulates parietal cells to release HCl” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 23.4 The Stomach, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-4-the-stomach)
