A melanocyte is a cell that produces the pigment melanin, which gives hair and skin their color. Melanin also helps protect the living cells of the epidermis, the outermost layer of the skin, from ultraviolet (UV) radiation damage. In the skin, melanocytes are found scattered throughout the stratum basale, the deepest layer of the epidermis. Melanocytes arise from the neural crest, a tissue that migrates through the embryo and develops into parts of both nervous and other tissues. Inside the melanocyte, melanin is made and stored in melanosomes, which are structures wrapped in a membrane. Melanosomes carry melanin from melanocytes into keratinocytes, the most common cells of the epidermis. Sun exposure makes keratinocytes release chemicals that stimulate melanocytes, so more melanin is made and builds up in the keratinocytes. This buildup of melanin darkens the skin, producing a tan. The extra melanin also protects the DNA of epidermal cells from UV damage.
