An intervertebral disk firmly joins two neighboring vertebrae, the bones of the neck and back. The disk absorbs shocks from walking and running and lets the spine move a little. The disk's tough, fibrous outer ring is called the anulus fibrosus. Inside the ring is the nucleus pulposus, a soft, watery gel that resists squeezing.
