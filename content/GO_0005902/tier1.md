Microvilli are thin parts that stick out from a cell and give it more surface area. In the small intestine, microvilli give the lining a fuzzy look called the brush border. Each square millimeter of lining has about 200 million of them, so they greatly expand the surface area for absorption. Enzymes, proteins that speed up reactions, are fixed to the microvilli and finish breaking down carbohydrates and proteins.
