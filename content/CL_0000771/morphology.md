# Eosinophil: morphology profile (unreviewed)

What Bio Map draws for this cell type, and the source sentence behind each choice. Generated from the front matter; edit the front matter, not this list.

- **Size:** 10–12 µm diameter  
  “Eosinophils typically represent 2–4 percent of total leukocyte count. They are also 10–12 µm in diameter.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Nuclei:** 1  
  “a granular leukocyte with a nucleus that usually has two lobes connected by one or more slender threads of chromatin” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000771 eosinophil, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000771)
- **Nucleus shape:** bilobed  
  “a nucleus that usually has two lobes connected by one or more slender threads of chromatin” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000771 eosinophil, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000771)
- **Nucleus shape:** multilobed (2–3 lobes)  
  “The nucleus of the eosinophil will typically have two to three lobes” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Special structure:** one or more slender chromatin threads joining the nuclear lobes  
  “a nucleus that usually has two lobes connected by one or more slender threads of chromatin” (Cell Ontology, CL:0000771 eosinophil, http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000771)
- **Granules:** eosinophil granules; look: unknown; abundance: many; position: unknown  
  “Granular leukocytes contain abundant granules within the cytoplasm. They include neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** Its granules carry antihistamine molecules, so it can counter the histamine that basophils and mast cells release.  
  “The granules of eosinophils include antihistamine molecules, which counteract the activities of histamines, inflammatory chemicals produced by basophils and mast cells.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** Some of its granules hold molecules that are toxic to parasitic worms, which equips it against worm infections.  
  “Some eosinophil granules contain molecules toxic to parasitic worms, which can enter the body through the integument, or when an individual consumes raw or undercooked fish or meat.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** It can also engulf targets by phagocytosis, and does this best when antibodies have coated the target.  
  “Eosinophils are also capable of phagocytosis and are particularly effective when antibodies bind to the target and form an antigen-antibody complex.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets)
- **Structure and job:** Called by T cell cytokines to where parasitic worms have settled in the gut, it releases the contents of its granules when it arrives.  
  “some worms take up residence in the gastrointestinal tract. Eosinophils are attracted to the site by T cell cytokines, which release their granule contents upon their arrival.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.5 The Immune Response against Pathogens, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-5-the-immune-response-against-pathogens)
- **Structure and job:** It can latch onto a parasite that IgE antibody has labelled, binding it through an Fc receptor.  
  “Furthermore, if IgE labels the parasite, the eosinophils can bind to it by its Fc receptor.” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.5 The Immune Response against Pathogens, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-5-the-immune-response-against-pathogens)
- **Structure and job:** Like neutrophils, it engulfs large pathogens such as bacteria and fungi.  
  “Neutrophils and eosinophils are particularly important leukocytes that engulf large pathogens, such as bacteria and fungi.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.1 Innate Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-1-innate-immune-response)
- **Structure and job:** It produces extra inflammatory mediators that bring in more leukocytes.  
  “Eosinophils and basophils produce additional inflammatory mediators to recruit more leukocytes.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.1 Innate Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-1-innate-immune-response)
