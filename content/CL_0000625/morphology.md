- **Structure and job:** Its CD8 marker doubles as an adhesion molecule, binding the MHC molecule directly so the T cell stays in close contact with the antigen-presenting cell.  
  “mature T cells express either the CD4 marker or the CD8 marker, but not both. These markers are cell adhesion molecules that keep the T cell in close contact with the antigen-presenting cell by directly binding to the MHC molecule (to a different part of the molecule than does the antigen).” (OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 21.3 The Adaptive Immune Response: T lymphocytes and Their Functional Types, https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/21-3-the-adaptive-immune-response-t-lymphocytes-and-their-functional-types)
- **Structure and job:** Being CD8-positive, it engages antigen held in MHC class I molecules on antigen-presenting cells and, once stimulated, becomes a cytotoxic T lymphocyte that kills infected cells by apoptosis and releases cytokines.  
  “In contrast, CD8+ cells engage antigen-embedded MHC I molecules on APCs and are stimulated to become cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), which directly kill infected cells by apoptosis and emit cytokines to amplify the immune response.” (OpenStax Biology 2e, 42.2 Adaptive Immune Response, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/42-2-adaptive-immune-response)
