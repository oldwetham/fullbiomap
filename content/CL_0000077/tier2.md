Mesothelial cells secrete serous fluid, a lubricant that reduces rubbing and friction between organs. Mesothelial cells form the mesothelium, the tissue that covers the major body cavities. The mesothelium is a simple squamous epithelium: a single layer of flat, scale-like cells. The mesothelium forms the smooth, protective surface layer of the serous membranes, which line body cavities and internal organs. In the pleura, the serous membrane covering the lungs, mesothelial cells on both of its layers secrete pleural fluid that lubricates the surfaces. This lubrication reduces friction between the two layers during breathing and helps keep the lungs against the chest wall. Mesothelial cells also closely monitor the level of pleural fluid. Around the heart, the mesothelium secretes serous fluid that reduces friction as the heart contracts. Mesothelial cells develop from the mesoderm, the middle layer of cells in the embryo.
