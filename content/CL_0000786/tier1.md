A plasma cell releases antibodies, proteins that protect the body by binding to foreign objects such as bacteria and viruses. Plasma cells often move to the bone marrow, the tissue inside bones where blood cells develop. Plasma cells form from activated B cells, immune cells whose main job is making antibodies. A plasma cell is packed with rough endoplasmic reticulum, the part of a cell that makes proteins.
